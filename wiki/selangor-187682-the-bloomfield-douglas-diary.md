# Selangor, 1876–82: the Bloomfield Douglas diary

P. Cameron's 1907 article in the Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society describes a suite of new Hymenoptera species collected at Kuching, Borneo, spanning the families Braconidae, Ichneumonidae, and Vespidae, along with two new genera. The work is a taxonomic contribution grounded in specimens from the Sarawak Museum, with Cameron providing detailed morphological diagnoses and establishing new generic and specific names for insects previously unrecorded from the region.

## Summary

Cameron's article is organized into four sections, each addressing a different group of hymenopterans. The first and largest section deals with *Iphiaulax* (Braconidae), a genus Cameron identifies as the most species-rich of Hymenoptera in Malaya. He groups the species by the morphology of the plate on the base of the second abdominal segment, describing nine species in total—eight new to science and one previously described by Smith. The diagnostic characters he employs are highly detailed, covering abdominal striation patterns, keel morphology, malar space proportions, wing venation, and pubescence density.

The second section introduces two new ichneumonid genera—*Hytophatnus* and *Aulojoppa*—each with a type species, followed by descriptions of additional species in the tribes Cryptinae and Pimplinae. Cameron's generic diagnoses emphasize combinations of characters such as clypeal shape, metanotal areolation, mandibular dentition, and ovipositor length. The third section describes two new species of *Mellinus* (a genus of wasps), providing a key to separate them from the previously known Bornean species *M. crabriformis*. The final, brief section covers Vespidae, describing one new species of *Icaria* and recording additional localities for three previously described species.

Throughout, Cameron relies on specimens collected by John Hewitt, Curator of the Sarawak Museum, with collection dates spanning the full year (January through December) and localities including Kuching, Sadong, Marup, Transan, and Merdang. The article reflects the taxonomic conventions of early 20th-century entomology, with emphasis on external morphology and comparative description against allied species.

### Key Findings

- Eight new species of *Iphiaulax* described: *cariniceps*, *octofoveatus*, *alienatus*, *extraneus*, *eurythecus*, *pallidiorbitalis*, *longiceps*, *lineativentris*, and *declinatus*, with body lengths ranging from 9 to 15 mm and ovipositor lengths from 11 to 42 mm (pp. 2–11).
- Two new ichneumonid genera established: *Hytophatnus* (type *H. lineatus*, 9 mm) and *Aulojoppa* (type *A. spilocephala*, 10 mm), each defined by combinations of clypeal, metanotal, and mandibular characters (pp. 12–15).
- Two new species of *Mellinus* described: *M. nigrolineatus* (10 mm, collected 14 January) and *M. nigromaculatus* (8 mm, collected 13 November), bringing the total Bornean *Mellinus* fauna to three species (pp. 22–25).
- One new Vespidae species described: *Icaria parvimaculata* (6 mm to end of second abdominal segment), collected at Marup in May (p. 25).
- Collection data spans all twelve months of the year at Kuching, with additional records from Sadong, Marup, Transan, and Merdang, indicating year-round activity of these hymenopteran groups in the region (pp. 2–26).

### Conclusion

Cameron's article represents a substantial increment to the known hymenopteran fauna of Borneo, adding at least thirteen new species and two new genera to the regional inventory. The work underscores the taxonomic richness of the *Iphiaulax* genus in the Malay region and provides the first descriptions of several ichneumonid lineages from the area, establishing a baseline for subsequent faunal studies.
