# The answer of Pasai

This is a short note by H. N. Ridley describing the tiger beetles (Cicindelidae) of the Malay Peninsula, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*, No. 38 (1902), pp. 129–131.

## Summary

Ridley divides the Malay tiger beetles into two ecological groups: "jungle-tiger beetles" (genera *Tricondyla* and *Collyris*) and "road-tiger beetles" (genera *Cicindela* and *Heptadonta*). He describes *Tricondyla aptera* as a wingless, deep blue beetle that mimics the large ant *Camponotus gigas*, and notes its rarity in the peninsula compared to its abundance in New Guinea and the Solomon Islands (p. 129). Three species of *Collyris* are treated: *C. dolens*, *C. filiformis*, and *C. apicalis*, all small, slender, blue or violet beetles found in sunny jungle clearings (p. 130).

For the road-tiger beetles, Ridley describes *Cicindela aurulenta* as the commonest species, abundant on sandy roads across the peninsula up to 3000 feet in altitude, and *C. fuliginosa* as a smaller, less common species with a dark brown key pattern on cream elytra (p. 130). *Heptadonta analis* is noted as a plain dark blue-green form with a wider distribution extending to Bombay, Java, and Sumatra (pp. 130–131). Ridley concludes that further species, particularly from hill regions, remain to be described (p. 131).
