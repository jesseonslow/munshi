# Public art, nationalism and national unification in Malaya/Malaysia

This short note by William R. Roff examines the role of public art in nationalist and national unification in Malaya/Malaysia, prompted by his encounter with the work of Guyanese artist Denis Williams.

Roff observes that the terms "art" and "artist" are entirely absent from his own *Origins of Malay Nationalism* (1967) and, to his knowledge, from the broader literature on Malaysian history and society that has followed. Drawing on Partha Mitter's study of art and nationalism in colonial India, he argues that colonial Malaya saw no attempt to confront threats to Malay or common Malayan identity through public art. He identifies a modest shift in the early post-Merdeka period, pointing to the artistic facades of Muzium Negara and the old Dewan Bahasa, and the statue in the grounds of Parliament House, as early expressions of a shared national identity. More recently, he notes the sculptures in Putrajaya, discussed by Muhizam Mustafa, though he flags that Mustafa frames their unifying effect in terms of a "modern Islamic Malay state" (p. 100). Roff concludes that the larger question remains open and invites further exploration by others better equipped to address it.
