# Public art, nationalism and national unification in Malaya/Malaysia

This is a brief personal essay by William R. Roff, published in the "The paper I never wrote" occasional series in JMBRAS Vol. 85, Part 2 (2012), pp. 99–101.

## Summary

Roff recounts his late-1960s visits to Pekan, Pahang, where he gained access to the official records of the Pejabat Sultan (from 1912 to the post-war period) and the papers of the Majlis Anggota Islam, a body formed in the late 1920s under Sultan Abdullah and headed by Tengku Besar Sulaiman. He describes the informal audiences with the Sultan, the physical condition of the archives, and the social context of his work in the town (pp. 99–100).

The piece culminates in a reflection on the concept of *nasehat*—the advice or counsel a sultan delivered during visits to outlying kampungs—which carried a form of sultanate authority distinct from but comparable to *titah*. Roff explains that the paper he ultimately never wrote would have explored "distance and authority" in a Malay sultanate, encompassing both the social gulf between ruler and subjects and the symbolic spatial distances embodied in rituals such as *sembah* (p. 100). A closing note indicates that both record deposits are now held at Arkib Negara Pahang (p. 101).
