# The Malay coins of Malacca

E. M. Satow's "Essay Towards a Bibliography of Siam (Continued)" (1886) is a reference compilation cataloguing periodical literature, linguistic works, and cartographic materials relating to Siam. The text provided comprises Parts II through IV of the essay, covering periodicals and proceedings of learned societies, language and grammar works, and maps.

## Summary

This is a continuation of Satow's systematic bibliography of Siam-related publications, here presenting the sections on periodicals and learned society proceedings, language works, and maps. The periodicals section (entries 184–229a) is the most extensive, indexing articles from French, English, and Dutch journals spanning the late eighteenth century through 1886. It draws heavily on French colonial and missionary sources — *Annales de l'Extrême Orient*, *Annales de la Propagation de la Foi*, *Excursions et Reconnaissances*, and the *Bulletin de la Société de Géographie* — alongside English-language outlets such as the *Bangkok Recorder*, the *Siam Repository*, the *Journal of the Indian Archipelago and Eastern Asia*, and the *Asiatic Journal and Monthly Register*. The entries are annotated with page references, author names, and brief descriptive notes, making the work a practical research guide for scholars seeking primary accounts of Siamese history, religion, trade, and geography.

The language section (entries 230–249) catalogues grammars, dictionaries, and comparative linguistic studies of the Siamese (Thai) language, from the 1810 *Comparative Vocabulary* of Burma, Malayu, and T'hai languages through Ewald's 1881 *Grammatik der T'ai-oder Siamesischen Sprache*. It includes works by missionaries (Pallegoix, McDonald, Caswell), colonial officials (Low, von Bergen), and European philologists (Bastian, Cust, Forbes, Kuhn, Lepsius). The maps section (Part IV) lists cartographic works from Kiepert (1867) through Hallett's 1886 survey map, including a Jesuit map from the seventeenth century and various government and missionary-produced charts.

The bibliography reflects the state of Siamese studies as it stood in the mid-1880s, a period of intense European interest driven by French colonial expansion in Indochina, the proposed Kra Isthmus canal, and missionary activity. Satow's annotations occasionally flag reprints, cross-references, and the relationship between original and secondary publications, demonstrating careful attention to the documentary trail.

### Key Findings

- The periodicals section indexes over 45 distinct publications, with the *Annales de l'Extrême Orient* (6 vols., 1878–84) alone yielding more than 40 individual entries across its volumes (pp. 163–166).
- The *Bangkok Recorder* (1865–1867, 20 volumes) is indexed in detail, revealing a rich vein of contemporary reporting on Siamese history, tribute missions, religious affairs, and the 1855 U.S. treaty of amity (pp. 168–170).
- The language section documents 20 works spanning 1810 to 1886, including Pallegoix's *Grammatica Linguae Thai* (Bangkok, 1850) and his *Dictionarium Linguae Thai* (Paris, 1854), which remain foundational references for Siamese philology (pp. 169–171).
- Captain James Low's contributions appear across multiple entries: his *Grammar of the Thai Language* (Calcutta, 1828), his papers on Siamese government and literature in *Asiatic Researches* (vol. XX), and his article on the Laws of Muang Thai in the *Journal of the Indian Archipelago* (vol. I, pp. 328–426) (pp. 165, 169, 172).
- The maps section includes a seventeenth-century Jesuit map (*Le Royaume de Siam avec les Royaumes qui luy sont tributaires*, Amsterdam, Ottens) in which the Malay Peninsula is labelled "Malacca ou Sornau," alongside a 4-sheet map of Siam and dependencies constructed from Siamese government surveys of 1867–1868 (p. 173).
- The bibliography extends to 1886, incorporating the latest available material including Hallett's exploration survey for a railway connection between India, Siam, and China (Royal Geographical Society Proceedings, January 1886) and Luzzati's article on the present and future of the Kingdom of Siam (*Bollettino della Società Geografica Italiana*, September 1886) (pp. 175–176).

### Conclusion

Satow's bibliography serves as a comprehensive index to the European-language literature on Siam available by the mid-1880s, reflecting the convergence of French colonial scholarship, British administrative interest, and missionary documentation. Its value lies not in original argument but in its systematic recovery and cross-referencing of scattered periodical articles, making it an indispensable starting point for researchers working on nineteenth-century Siamese history, linguistics, and cartography.

## Context

- The work draws on the holdings and knowledge of the Royal Asiatic Society of Great Britain and Ireland, of which Satow was a member and whose *Journal* published the original essay.
- The bibliography's structure and coverage reflect the historiographical priorities of the 1880s: French Indochinese expansion, the Kra canal debate, and the documentation of Siamese Buddhism and language by missionaries and philologists.
