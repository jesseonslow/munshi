# A brief account of several countries surrounding Prince of Wales’s Island with their production. Recd. from Captain Leight {sic} Enclosed in Lord Cornwallis’s letter to Mr. Dundas, dated 7th January, 1789.

This is a brief geographical and commercial account of the countries surrounding Prince of Wales's Island ([Penang](./penang.md)), received from Captain [Francis Light](./francis-light.md) and enclosed in Lord Cornwallis's letter to Mr. Dundas, dated 7 January 1789. It was communicated to the [Journal](./journal.md) by C. E. Wurtzburg (p. 123).

## Summary

The document provides a concise survey of approximately twenty ports, rivers, and territories around the Strait of [Malacca](./melaka.md), the [Malay](./malay.md) Peninsula, [Sumatra](./sumatra.md), and [Borneo](./borneo.md), noting for each their political status, principal produce, and trade connections. Entries cover [Siam](./thailand.md), Chantebon, Chia, Sangora, Pattany, Ligore, Tringano, [Pahang](./pahang.md), Jahore, Rheo, Andiagery, Siak, Battubar, Langkatt, Achee Pedir, [Acheen](./aceh.md), Salang, Queda, Pirac, Salengore, [Malacca](./melaka.md), Mergui and Tavoy, Pallamban, Pontiano, Mompava, and Sacadanea (pp. 123–126).

The account reflects the commercial and strategic interests of the British [East India Company](./east-[india](./india.md)-company.md) in the late 1780s, recording annual export values (e.g., Achee Pedir's 4–500,000 Spanish dollars in [pepper](./pepper.md) and betel-nut; Pallamban's 40,000 peculs of tin), Dutch factory locations, and the political disruptions caused by Burmese invasions and Siamese conquests (pp. 123–126).
