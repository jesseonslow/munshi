# Founding an ethnic Chinese business empire in colonial Asia: the strategic alliances of Major Oei Tiong Ham, 1895–1905

This article by Peter Post examines the strategic alliances through which Oei Tiong Ham built his ethnic Chinese business empire in colonial Asia during the period 1895–1905.

## Summary

Post traces how Oei Tiong Ham constructed a transnational business network spanning Singapore, Penang, and Java, arguing that his success rested on a deliberately cosmopolitan and transcultural strategic positioning rather than allegiance to any single nation, empire, or ethnic identity. A key strategy was the deliberate unification of peranakan and singkeh communities, as well as modern and traditional elements of Indies Chinese society, which Post contends was necessary for the Chinese business community to compete with dominant European firms and navigate Dutch colonial anti-Chinese policies (p. 54).

The article also addresses Oei Tiong Ham's complex relationship with Chinese nationalism and the *kaum muda* movement, noting that his queue-cutting in December 1901 was likely a business calculation rather than an ideological commitment, and that he was subsequently elevated to an icon of Chinese modernism despite his own discomfort with being drawn into a single political camp (p. 54). Post's central argument is that Oei Tiong Ham was fundamentally a pragmatic tycoon whose transcultural alliances served the singular purpose of creating the largest ethnic Chinese conglomerate in the world.
