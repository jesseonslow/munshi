# Some First-Hand Accounts of the Japanese Occupation

This is a 1926 account by Dato Sedia Raja Abdullah describing the "Mandi Ayer Gawar" (Bathing in Holy Water) ceremony, a Malay ritual for warding off epidemics, republished in P.H. Kratoska's 2024 compilation of first-hand accounts (p. 102).

## Summary

The text opens with a brief travel narrative describing a journey through the Rompin area, including observations of quartz-porphyry rock exposures, an encounter with ten Jakun people, and a boat passage to Mersing (p. 102). The main body then details the "Mandi Ayer Gawar" ceremony as performed by a *Dukun* (medicine-man) when an epidemic threatens a village. The ritual involves a tray of specific plant materials—*Rotan Tikus*, *Periok Kera* (a pitcher plant), *Buloh Gading*, and *Kayu Alai*—selected according to the type of epidemic, along with incense, toasted rice, and cloth strips (p. 103).

The *Dukun* selects a leading jar (*Rebok Tua*) from among earthen jars placed by villagers, into which three copper coins are dropped; the remaining jars receive one coin each. After reciting incantations and Arabic prayers, the materials are left overnight for the invoked saints to work upon them. The following morning, the water is poured over villagers' heads, and each receives a *Benang Sempurna* (a three-coloured thread), flexible lead bound in black cloth, and plant symbols to be placed on their gates and doors—visible signs to the spirit king of the epidemic that the villagers are his subjects and must not be attacked (p. 104). Abdullah notes that even the professional *Pawang* cannot rationally explain the ceremony's elements, which are accepted blindly, a feature he identifies as a key difficulty for research into Malay magic (p. 104).
