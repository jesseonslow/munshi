# Hindustani loan-words in Malay

This is a short note by R. O. Winstedt, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (No. 76, 1917), extracting a list of Malay words of Hindustani derivation from a 1902 article by Dr. Ph. S. van Ronkel.

## Summary

Winstedt explains that van Ronkel's article appeared in a French-language brochure presented to the Congress of Orientalists at Hanoi, and that it had escaped his notice when he compiled his *Malay Grammar* and *English-Malay Dictionary*. He reproduces a list of approximately twenty words with their Hindustani (and often Sanskrit) etymologies, including *achita* (unmilled rice), *artal* (orpiment), *akas* (firmament), *bandahari* (chief treasurer), *churi* (steal), *chulim* (opium fill), *kapis* (cotton), *kanji* (rice broth), *kunchi* (key), *kuli* (paid labourer), *gudang* (store), and *muti* (pearl) (p. 67–68).

The note also records a few corrections and qualifications: *pelangking* (palanquin) is identified as a Portuguese coinage from Sanskrit *paryanka*; *lagam* (horse-bit), which Winstedt had previously listed as Hindustani in his Grammar, is reclassified as a Persian loan-word that Malay may have borrowed from Persian rather than directly from Hindustani (p. 68).
