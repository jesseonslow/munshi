# Magindano

C.A. Gibson-Hill's brief note traces the historical usage of the obsolete variant "Magindano" for the Philippine island Mindanao, identifying Captain Thomas Forrest as its likely originator.

## Summary

Gibson-Hill addresses a concern raised in C.E. Wurtzburg's 1954 biography of Sir Stamford Raffles regarding the precise position of the name *Magindano* in the island's nomenclature. He establishes that the standard form *Mindanao* (or *Mindano*) was used by early writers including Combes (1667), Dampier (1686), and all eighteenth-century authors up to Dalrymple. The variant *Magindano* was introduced by Captain Thomas Forrest (1775–1776), who offered an etymological gloss deriving it from *Mag* (related to), *in* (country), and *dano* (lake). Forrest's form was adopted by Milburn (1813) and John Hunt (1812), and persisted in Singapore newspapers for roughly fifty years, though Crawfurd and Wallace used the older form. Gibson-Hill concludes that subsequent writers reverted to the original *Mindanao*, and that the variant was almost certainly launched by Forrest, whose terminology shows other personal variations (p. 184).
