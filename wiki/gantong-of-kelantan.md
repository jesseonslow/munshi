# Gantong of Kelantan

This short note by Anker Rentse (1933) describes several historical *gantang* (rice measures) from Kelantan, documenting their inscriptions, materials, and volumes relative to the modern standard.

## Summary

Rentse examines four measures: a bronze *gantang* found near the mouth of the Sokor river, inscribed with the date 1222 A.H. (1807 A.D.) and attributed to Sultan Muhammad I (Raja Muhammad I, Marhum Mandul); a wooden *gantang* of *Chengal* with a brass band and tin *tera* (seal) dated 1269 A.H. (1852 A.D.), from the reign of Sultan Muhammad II; a coconut-shell *chupak* of the same period; and a tin *gantang* dated 1319 A.H. (1901 A.D.). The inscription on the bronze *gantang* reads "Ini-lah gantang (perbelanjaan?) sukatan (Sultan al'adil?)" and is translated as "a currency and measure," reflecting the historical use of rice as a medium of exchange in Kelantan (p. 241). The older measures are all smaller than the present standard (1 gallon = 1 *gantang* = 4 *chupak* = 8 *ling*), ranging from 6.6 to 7 pints against the modern 8 pints (p. 243). Rentse also notes that the Rajas affixed their *tera* seals to the top of measures to detect tampering, and that the *gantang* in Kelantan is used not only for rice but also for meat, fish, and salt (p. 244).
