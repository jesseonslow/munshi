# A history of Malaya

D. F. A. Hervey, writing in 1884 for the Journal of the Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, presented a complete English translation of François Valentyn's early eighteenth-century Dutch account of Malacca under VOC rule. The text serves as a primary source for understanding the political, commercial, and cultural landscape of the Straits of Malacca as perceived by a Dutch colonial administrator writing circa 1700–1717.

## Summary

The translation covers Valentyn's description of Malacca's physical geography, fortifications, administrative structure, and commercial significance under the Dutch East India Company. Valentyn draws on rare Malay-language genealogical manuscripts written in Arabic script—particularly the *Kitab Hang Tuah* and the *Soolalet Issalathina*—to reconstruct the pre-colonial history of the Malay kingdom from its mythical origins through the Portuguese conquest of 1511. The narrative traces the succession of Malay sultans from Sri Tri Buwana (c. 1160) through Sultan Mahmud Shah, whose flight to Johor in 1511 marked the end of the Malaccan sultanate and the beginning of the Johor dynasty.

The account of the Portuguese arrival is particularly detailed, narrating the failed assassination plot against Jorge de Sequeira in 1509 and Afonso de Albuquerque's conquest in 1511. Valentyn also provides extensive lists of VOC officials—governors, supercargos, shahbandars, attorneys-general, and treasurers—spanning from 1641 to 1717, which constitute a valuable administrative record of the Dutch government at Malacca. The text further describes the subordinate factories at Perak, Kedah, and Oudong Salang, noting the difficulties the Company faced in maintaining these outposts amid local hostility.

### Key Findings

- Gross revenue of Malacca in 1669: 74,958.18 guilders, derived from a 10 per cent import duty, 3 per cent export duty, and minor taxes (p. 4).
- 116 Javanese vessels arrived in Malacca in 1669, in addition to Danish, Portuguese, and Moorish vessels (p. 4).
- The Portuguese conquest of 1511 yielded a royal fifth (*quinto*) of 200,000 ducats (p. 28).
- The town's population had declined from 12,000 souls in former times to no more than 200–300 families by Valentyn's time (p. 3).
- The Malay kingdom at Malacca endured 252 years under Malay kings before the Portuguese conquest (p. 29).
- The Perak factory was broken up after all its personnel were murdered in 1651 (p. 8).

### Conclusion

Valentyn's account, as rendered by Hervey, provides a comprehensive snapshot of Malacca at the turn of the eighteenth century—its diminished population, its continued commercial importance as a transit point, and its layered colonial history from Malay sultanate to Portuguese to Dutch rule. The work's enduring value lies in its preservation of Malay genealogical traditions and its detailed administrative records of VOC governance.

## Context

- Hervey's translation draws on Valentyn's original Dutch text and supplements it with notes referencing documents in the Malacca Archives, as well as cross-references to Leyden's *Malay Annals* and Crawfurd's works.
- The translation was undertaken because an earlier partial rendering in Logan's *Journal* (Vol. IV) was incomplete and unreliable, making this the first trustworthy complete English rendering of Valentyn's Malacca chapter.
