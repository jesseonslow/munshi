# The ferns of the Malay Peninsula

This is a brief notice by Archdeacon Hose and A. M. Skinner, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (December 1879, No. 4, pp. xxii–xxv), recording the reception of Professor Adolf Nordenskiöld and the Swedish *Vega* expedition by the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, extracted from the *Straits Times* of 4 December 1879.

## Summary

The document preserves the closing portion of a speech (attributed to Archdeacon Hose) congratulating Nordenskiöld on the *Vega*'s successful circumnavigation of Europe and Asia via the North-East Passage, and projecting the commercial, meteorological, and humanitarian benefits of the voyage (pp. xxii–xxiii). Nordenskiöld's reply contrasts the Arctic conditions at Cape Tscheliskin with the tropical climate of the Straits Settlements, affirms the future navigability of the ice-free channel from the Jenisei to Bering, and credits English seafaring enthusiasm for encouraging further polar exploration (pp. xxiv–xxv). The notice closes with a brief record of Captain Palander exhibiting charts of the "silent sea" and Nordenskiöld presenting a quartz specimen from the northernmost point of Asia to Dr. Dennys for the museum (p. xxv).
