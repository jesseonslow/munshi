# The tiger-breed families

Zainal Abidin bin Ahmad's short note documents the belief among certain families in Jempul, Malacca, that their deceased members transform into tigers after burial.

## Summary

The article describes the full cycle of the transformation: tigers gather around the house of a dying family member, the corpse is carried to the forest after burial, and a new tiger appears bearing the physical marks and gait of the dead person, recognisable by name. Ahmad illustrates the belief with two personal anecdotes—one involving his grandmother calling a dead friend named Faseh to drive off buffaloes, and another in which a woman's dream of her wounded brother is confirmed by the discovery of a tiger killed by a spring-gun with a matching chest wound (pp. 37–38). He also notes that these family-tigers visit relatives during Hari Raya and feast in kitchens, and identifies two particularly revered graveyards, *Kubor Nesan* and *Kubor Leban*, as sources of *Harimau Keramat* under the lordship of *Dato Paroi* of Gunong Angsi (pp. 38–39). The note closes by distinguishing these post-mortem transformations from the were-tiger belief (*harimau jadi-jadian*) attributed to the Korinchi of Sumatra, where the power is indicated by the absence of the philtrum (p. 39).
