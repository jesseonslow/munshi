# A note on Sai

[W. Linehan](./w-linehan.md)'s brief note (1947) corrects a misattribution regarding the historical territory of Sai in [Patani](./patani.md) and clarifies the [Malay](./malay.md) distinction between *Sai* and *[Siam](./thailand.md)*.

## Summary

Linehan responds to earlier references by V. B. C. Baker and [Anker Rentse](./anker-rentse.md), who had credited his "[History](./history.md) of [Pahang](./pahang.md)" (1936) with a mention of Sai that does not exist in that work (p. 104). He further argues that Baker's conjecture—that the [Malay](./malay.md) term *orang Siam* referred to "the men of Sai"—is incorrect, since the Malays consistently distinguished *Sai* (a specific territory within Siam) from *[Siam](./thailand.md)* (the country as a whole).

Linehan nonetheless concedes that Baker was likely right in suggesting that miners from Sai, entering Pahang via the Galas river and Pulai, played a significant role in developing gold-fields in Ulu [Pahang](./pahang.md). He supports this with the place-name *Gua Sai* ("the Cave of Sai") in the Mukim of Gua, near the gold-field of the river Tui (p. 104). The note closes with a brief historical note that Sai was once part of the kingdom of [Patani](./patani.md) and became a separate petty state following the 1830–1831 rebellion against Siam.
