# A note on Sai

W. Linehan's brief note (1947) corrects a misattribution regarding the historical territory of Sai in Patani and clarifies the Malay distinction between *Sai* and *Siam*.

## Summary

Linehan responds to earlier references by V. B. C. Baker and Anker Rentse, who had credited his "History of Pahang" (1936) with a mention of Sai that does not exist in that work (p. 104). He further argues that Baker's conjecture—that the Malay term *orang Siam* referred to "the men of Sai"—is incorrect, since the Malays consistently distinguished *Sai* (a specific territory within Siam) from *Siam* (the country as a whole).

Linehan nonetheless concedes that Baker was likely right in suggesting that miners from Sai, entering Pahang via the Galas river and Pulai, played a significant role in developing gold-fields in Ulu Pahang. He supports this with the place-name *Gua Sai* ("the Cave of Sai") in the Mukim of Gua, near the gold-field of the river Tui (p. 104). The note closes with a brief historical note that Sai was once part of the kingdom of Patani and became a separate petty state following the 1830–1831 rebellion against Siam.
