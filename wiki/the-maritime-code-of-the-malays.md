# The maritime code of the Malays

This is a brief extract from the *Straits Times* (4 December 1879), reproduced in the JSBRAS, recording the reception of Professor Adolf Erik Nordenskjöld and the Swedish *Vega* expedition by the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, with speeches by Archdeacon Hose and A. M. Skinner and a reply by Nordenskjöld himself (pp. xxii–xxv).

## Summary

The document preserves the text of a formal dinner at which the Society honoured the members of the *Vega* expedition on their homeward passage through Singapore. The host's address (attributed to Archdeacon Hose) congratulates Nordenskjöld on solving the North-East Passage problem and speculates on the commercial, meteorological, and humanitarian benefits that may follow, including the possibility of Arctic weather stations extending the range of forecasts (p. xxiii). Nordenskjöld's reply contrasts the frozen, uninhabited coast of Cape Tscheliskin with the lush southern climate of the Straits Settlements, describes the ice-free channel from the Jenisei to Bering Strait through which the *Vega* sailed, and expresses confidence that the route will soon be used commercially (pp. xxiv–xxv). The extract closes with a note that Captain Palander exhibited charts of the "silent sea" and that Nordenskjöld presented a specimen of quartz from the northernmost point of Asia to Dr. Dennys for the Society's museum (p. xxv).
