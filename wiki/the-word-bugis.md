# The word Bugis

This document comprises two Notes & Queries items published in JMBRAS Vol. 89, No. 2 (2016), the first being a query by Russell Jones on the etymology of the Malay word "Bugis" and the second by Paul Kratoska on the identity of the author of *A Yellow Sleuth* (1931).

## Summary

Russell Jones poses a question prompted by an inquiry from the Oxford English Dictionary: while the Bugis people refer to themselves as "Ugi" or "Wugi" in their own language, the English form "Bugis" is taken from Malay, and Jones asks where the Malay form acquired its final "-s" (p. 139). The editor notes that a plural explanation is plausible but that the name appears in 19th-century British documents as "Bugesse," which complicates that account.

Paul Kratoska, Publishing Director at NUS Press, recounts how he discovered a 1934 Parisian French translation of a book originally published in English in 1931 as *A Yellow Sleuth: Being the Autobiography of "Nor Nalla"*, attributed to a Detective-Sergeant in the Federated Malay States Police (pp. 139–140). Contemporary reviews noted the difficulty of distinguishing fact from fiction and the author's exceptional command of English. Kratoska reports that NUS Press is planning a new edition and invites members to help identify the true author (p. 140).
