# The “palang” II: Three further notes

This is a three-part short note by Tom Harrisson (1966) documenting additional material on the *palang*, a penile bar device used in Borneo, following his earlier 1964 article in the same journal.

## Summary

The note records three items: (i) a 1954 letter from Cranstone at the British Museum confirming that H. Ling Roth deposited four Kayan *utang* specimens in 1895, with a brief description of their use (p. 172); (ii) a pre-1890 Kayan tool called *katiputin* from the Belaga-Baloi area, consisting of two hardwood sticks and a twisted iron rod, used to perforate the glans penis horizontally during the operation (p. 173); and (iii) a calcinated urethral stone ("penolith") weighing 26 grams, removed from an Iban man in the Balleh area, which had formed around a 21 mm iron *palang* bar left in situ (p. 173–174). Harrisson notes that such secondary complications are widespread among older men in the *palang* zone but rarely reported to outside doctors due to cultural reticence.
