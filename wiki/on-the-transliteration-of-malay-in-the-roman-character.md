# On the transliteration of Malay in the roman character

This is a short note by Anker Rentse presenting two Kelantan folktales connected to a deluge narrative, published in the *Journal of the Malayan Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* in 1931 (pp. 141–142).

## Summary

Rentse introduces the tales by referencing a major flood in Kelantan in 1926, which local elders described as unprecedented in living memory. He then cites W. Skeat's *Fables and Folktales of an Eastern Forest* (1901), which records a Malayan deluge story in which animal fights—culminating in a fight between dogs and cats—trigger a catastrophic flood that drowns the entire population of Kelantan except a few firewood collectors in the hills (p. 141).

Rentse then presents a second version told to him in Kota Bharu by Che Ahamad, a bedari of Tengku Khalid. In this variant, the dog-and-cat fight provokes divine anger, and Tuan Allah hurls the mountain Gunong Noring from Perak into Kelantan, killing all festival attendees except a single pregnant woman who becomes the sole survivor (p. 141). The note concludes with a local legend about a *berhala* (a horseman figure) atop Gunong Noring whose poisonous breath was neutralised through a seven-day *main peteri* seance involving the sacrifice of a pink buffalo (p. 142).
