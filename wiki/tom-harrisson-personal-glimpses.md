# Tom Harrisson: personal glimpses

This is a personal obituary by Professor Neville Haile commemorating [Tom Harrisson](./tom-harrisson.md), the British anthropologist and naturalist who died in 1976 at the age of 64.

## Summary

Haile, who first encountered Harrisson through a 1949 BBC broadcast on the Kelabit people of Sarawak—a broadcast that prompted him to apply for a posting to Sarawak—recalls several personal episodes, including Harrisson's dramatic courtroom demonstration that secured the acquittal of two Kelabit men wrongly convicted of murder (p. 145). He then [surveys](./surveys.md) Harrisson's major scientific contributions: the discovery of prehistoric remains at Niah [Caves](./caves.md), the expansion of the [Sarawak](./sarawak.md) Museum, and ongoing research on birds, orangutans, and the rare lizard *Lanthanotus* (p. 145).

Haile characterises Harrisson as a complex, witty, and deeply passionate figure whose conservation work extended to his final public act, a plea for Malaysian forest preservation delivered at the invitation of the [MBRAS](./malaysian-branch-of-the-royal-asiatic-society.md) weeks before his death (p. 146). He notes that Harrisson's last administrative act concerning Sarawak was to convert his "Widows and Orphans Pension Fund" into a Trust for the Kelabit peoples, and laments that one who had survived wartime and exploration risks was killed in a road accident (p. 146).
