# Thomas Stamford Raffles’ Masonic career in Java: a new perspective on the British interregnum (1811–1816

## Abstract

This paper looks at ways in which Raffles’ membership of Dutch masonic lodges proved beneficial to his personal initiatives and professional career both in Java (1811–16) and subsequently as founder of Singapore (1819). Contradictory statements abound about the date of his admission to the Masonic Order. The dominant view is that he was initiated in the Lodge Virtutis et Artis Amici in 1813, a view based on a certificate dated to the masonic year 5813, which says that ‘T. Stamford Raffles’ had received the first two masonic degrees of Entered Apprentice and Fellow of the Craft in that lodge. Scrutiny of the certificate on the basis of a new photographic image, however, shows that the document itself and plans for the foundation of the lodge predate the British interregnum. At the same time, current interpretations of the masonic dating cannot be reconciled with Raffles’ elevation to the degree of Master Mason in the Lodge De Vriendschap in Surabaya on 5 July 1813. Establishing the correct date of Raffles’ initiation in September–October 1811 is therefore essential both for the reconstruction of his masonic career in Java, and an understanding of the special supranational character of the British interregnum itself.

## Summary

Jordaan and Carey undertake a forensic re-examination of the documentary evidence surrounding Thomas Stamford Raffles' admission to Freemasonry during the British interregnum in Java, challenging the long-standing Dutch masonic consensus that he was initiated in 1813 in a lodge supposedly founded for that purpose. Their central intervention is methodological: by subjecting Raffles' initiation certificate to new photographic analysis and cross-referencing it against the known movements of Lord Minto and the confirmed date of Raffles' elevation to Master Mason in Surabaya, they demonstrate that the 1813 dating is internally contradictory and that the correct window is September–October 1811, immediately following the British conquest and Raffles' appointment as Lieutenant-Governor.

The article then widens its lens to argue that this corrected chronology transforms our understanding of the British interregnum itself. The simultaneous initiation of Raffles and his Dutch Council colleague Muntinghe, performed in the presence of Lord Minto and Colonel Gillespie at the lodge on Engelhard's estate, is presented as the foundational act of a deliberately constructed Anglo-Dutch administrative partnership. The authors contend that the Java Council and the broader British interim government were in practice a "supranational masonic government," in which shared membership of the Order functioned as both a selection criterion and a bond of trust between British and Dutch officials across the judiciary, finance, public works, and cultural institutions.

The epilogue extends the argument beyond Java, suggesting that Raffles' Masonic credentials facilitated his later career: his interventions on behalf of Cranssen and Muntinghe before the Dutch Minister of Colonies Falck in 1817, his successful pitch to Lord Hastings in Calcutta in 1818, and his consent to the founding of a lodge in Singapore all appear more explicable when viewed through the lens of his established standing in the Order.

### Key Findings

- The paper on which Raffles' initiation certificate is printed predates the British interregnum, evidenced by the marginal name of Pieter Brouwer (Grand Secretary of the Grand East of Holland, 1801–1810) and a crossed-out reference to the Batavian Republic (1795–1806), indicating the lodge's founding was contemplated before 1811 (pp. 6–7).
- Raffles' elevation to Master Mason in the Lodge De Vriendschap, Surabaya, is confirmed by lodge minutes for 5 July 1813, independently corroborated by British Mason B. N. Powell's translation of those minutes; this date makes any 1813 initiation impossible since advancement cannot precede initiation (pp. 5–6, 13–14).
- Lord Minto departed Java aboard H.M.S. *Modeste* on 19 October 1811; since his presence at Raffles' initiation is attested by multiple sources including De Geus's 1930 letter to a British inquirer, the initiation must fall between 11 September 1811 (Raffles' appointment announced) and 19 October 1811 (pp. 11–12).
- Raffles paid 75 Spanish Dollars (equivalent to approximately £1,050 in present-day purchasing power) in April 1815 for the degree of Scots Master, and a further 150 Spanish Dollars (approximately £2,100) in March 1816 for the degree of Sovereign Prince of the Rose Croix, as recorded in the financial ledger of the Chapter of La Vertueuse (p. 17).
- By early 1814, 20 of the 61 members of Lodge La Vertueuse in Batavia were British, including Raffles, demonstrating the significant Anglo-Dutch composition of the lodge during the interregnum (p. 13).
- Of 59 Dutch jury members appointed in Batavia and its environs in April 1812, 30 could be identified as Masons; of 78 newly registered members of the Batavian Literary Society, at least 43 Dutch members were Masons (pp. 28, 32).

### Conclusion

Jordaan and Carey conclude that the British interregnum in Java was not merely a military occupation administered by a small British elite but a genuinely supranational project in which Freemasonry provided the connective tissue between British and Dutch officials. The corrected dating of Raffles' initiation to the autumn of 1811 reveals that the Masonic bond was established at the very inception of the new government, making it a constitutive rather than incidental feature of Anglo-Dutch cooperation in Java.

## Context

- The study draws primarily on the archives of the Cultureel Maconniek Centrum (CMC) in The Hague, including lodge membership lists, financial ledgers, and the initiation certificate itself (newly photographed), as well as issues of the British masonic periodical *The Pentagram* (1928, 1930, 1931) held at the United Grand Lodge library in London.
- Historiographically, the article corrects a persistent error in Dutch masonic scholarship (dating Raffles' initiation to 1813) and integrates British masonic sources that had been largely ignored by Dutch researchers, thereby offering a revised framework for understanding the political character of the British interregnum.
