# Stones and the Maloh of Indonesian West Borneo

This is a short note by Tom Harrisson presenting a newly available English translation of a passage from Ma Huan's 1433 A.D. account of Thailand, which documents a penis bead-insertion practice and its relevance to the Borneo *palang* (penis bar) tradition.

## Summary

Harrisson draws attention to a passage in Dr. J. V. G. Mills' 1970 Hakluyt Society edition of Ma Huan's *Ying-yai Sheng-lan*, which describes a Thai practice in which, upon reaching the age of twenty, men have the skin of the *membrum virile* incised and filled with tin beads (or gold beads with sand for the elite), a procedure performed by specialist practitioners (p. 89). The author argues this constitutes a clear parallel between Buddhist Thai and animist Dayak genital modification traditions, and that the Borneo bar device may have developed from such bead insertions or vice versa, since bead-like objects are sometimes permanently fixed onto the *palang* (p. 90).

The note situates this evidence within a broader pattern of penis surgery recorded from Borneo, the Philippines, Java, and Celebes, and suggests the mainland connection (possibly with Tantric Buddhism) is slightly strengthened, while calling for further study of diffusion routes via the Malay Peninsula and Sumatra (p. 90).
