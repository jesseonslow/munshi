# New Brenthidae from the Raffles Museum, with remarks on the Brenthid fauna of the Malay Peninsula

R. Kleine, a German entomologist based in Stettin, published this taxonomic paper in 1923 describing eight new taxa (seven species and one genus) of Brentid weevils from the Raffles Museum collection in Singapore, while also compiling the first comprehensive faunal list of Brentidae known from the southern Malay Peninsula. The work was prompted by Major Moulton, Director of the Raffles Museum, who sent a collection for identification, and was translated into English by Dr. Hanitsch from Kleine's original German manuscript (p. 271).

## Summary

The paper addresses a gap in the entomological record of the Malay Peninsula by describing several new species of broad-nosed weevils (family Brentidae) that had been collected in the region but remained unclassified. Kleine works through the material taxonomically, assigning each new species to its respective subfamily (Trachelizini, Arrhenodini, Ithystenini, Pseudocephalini) and providing detailed morphological descriptions of both sexes where available. The descriptions follow the conventions of early twentieth-century Coleoptera systematics, with emphasis on rostral structure, antennal joint morphology, elytral sculpture, and abdominal segmentation.

The most significant taxonomic contribution is the establishment of a new genus, *Holotrachelus*, within the Pseudocephalini, distinguished from the closely allied *Schizotrachelus* by the presence of teeth along the posterior margin of the eyes, a very short metarostrum, slender legs with thin tibiae, and a pilose apical abdominal segment (p. 277). Among the new species, *Diurus celsus* is particularly noteworthy for its unusual posteriorly produced head margin and the thickening of elytral ribs at the apex, characters Kleine states are unknown in any other species (p. 275). The paper closes with a faunal checklist of all Brentidae then known from Penang, Perak, and Siam, organized by subfamily, which serves as a baseline reference for the region's weevil fauna at that time (pp. 279–281).

### Key Findings

- Seven new species and one new genus are described: *Hypomiolisa demissa*, *Stratiorrhina concors*, *Diurus deruptus*, *Diurus celsus*, *Diurus completus*, *Holotrachelus comparabilis* (type of new genus *Holotrachelus*), and *Opisthenoplus commodus* (pp. 271–279).
- *Diurus completus* is identified as the largest species known to Kleine at the time, with a total length range of 21–60 mm and a prothorax width of 2–4.2 mm (p. 276).
- *Diurus celsus* was collected at elevations of 2,500–3,500 ft on Gunong Tahan (Pahang) and 2,000–2,790 ft on Gunong Angsi (Negri Sembilan), indicating a montane distribution (p. 275).
- *Hypomiolisa demissa* was collected at Bukit Kutu, Selangor, at an elevation of 3,457 ft in April 1915 by R. Hanitsch (p. 272).
- The faunal checklist enumerates approximately 45 species across nine subfamilies for the Penang–Perak–Siam region, with the Trachelizini subfamily being the most species-rich (pp. 279–281).
- *Opisthenoplus commodus* is recorded from both Java and Maxwell's Hill, Perak, representing a trans-Sundic distribution (p. 279).

### Conclusion

Kleine's paper establishes a foundational taxonomic record for Brentid weevils of the southern Malay Peninsula, demonstrating that the region harbours considerable endemic diversity within the family. The description of a new genus and multiple new species, several of which are restricted to montane habitats, underscores the faunal richness of the Peninsula's highland forests and provides a reference point against which subsequent collections could be measured.

## Context

- The paper reflects the colonial-era practice of European entomologists describing species from collections assembled in British Malaya and deposited in the Raffles Museum (Singapore), with types subsequently transferred to the British Museum in London.
- The faunal checklist (pp. 279–281) represents the first systematic compilation of Brentidae for the region and remains a useful historical baseline for biogeographical studies of weevil diversity in the Sunda Shelf.
