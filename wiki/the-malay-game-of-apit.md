# The Malay game of apit

This short note by Raja Samusah of Malay College, Kuala Kangsar, describes the rules of the Malay board game *sodok apit*, illustrated with four diagrams.

## Summary

The game is played by two players on a draughtsboard with 16 pieces of equal value arranged in two rows as in chess. Pieces move orthogonally (not diagonally) any number of squares, one at a time in turn. Captures are made by two methods: *sodo'*, in which a piece is moved into the empty square between two opposing pieces in a straight line to remove both (maximum two pieces per capture), and *apit*, in which one or more opposing pieces are enclosed on two opposite sides and removed (no upper limit on the number captured). A special rule stipulates that if a player deliberately places their own pieces in an *apit* position, the opponent cannot capture them until one of the enclosing pieces has been moved away and back (p. 138). A piece enclosed in a corner cannot be taken (p. 139). The article is presented bilingually in English and Malay, with the Malay text expanding slightly on the English description (pp. 138–139).
