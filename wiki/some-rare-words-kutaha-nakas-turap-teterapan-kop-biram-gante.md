# Some rare words: kutaha, nakas, turap, teterapan, kop, biram, ganteh, Seri Menanti

This is a brief philological note by R. O. Winstedt examining the meanings of several rare Malay words—*kutaha*, *nakas*, *turap*, *tētērapan*, *kop*, *biram*, *ganteh*, and *Sēri Mēnanti*—with reference to Wilkinson's *Dictionary of the Malay Language* and related texts (p. 49).

Winstedt discusses each word in turn, offering corrections and etymological connections. He links *tētērapan* to *turap* (meaning 'to dress, plaster, line'), identifies *kop* as a general term for 'dome, cupola' drawing on Achinese *khob*, and clarifies that *biram* refers not merely to an elephant but to a mythical two-headed snake, explaining the puzzle-ring term *chinchin patah biram* (p. 50). For *ganteh*, he argues the correct reading in the *Sēri Rama* folk-tale is 'thick in the centre; barrel-shaped,' corroborated by Wilkinson's *Sēri Mēnanti*, and notes that a later Malay philologist had misread the word as *ganti* (p. 50). Finally, he proposes that the toponym *Sēri Mēnanti* derives not from a rice variety but from *seri menganti* (the Javanese 'waiting-hall'), a more probable etymological source (p. 50).
