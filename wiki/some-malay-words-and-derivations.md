# Some Malay words and derivations

C. N. Maxwell's short note (pp. 182–183) offers critical responses to two recent articles in the *Journal of the Malayan Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*: A. W. Hamilton's "Some Malay words" and R. J. Bee's "Some Kelantan Place Names."

Maxwell objects to Hamilton's inclusion of Chinese-origin words in a list of Malay vocabulary, arguing that without a defined criterion for borrowing, the Malay lexicon would become a "cosmopolitan rubbish heap" (p. 182). He concedes that Hamilton's comparative work on cognates such as *kuda*, *kudira*, and *kudirei* is valuable, and notes that three of Hamilton's words (*lemusir*, *bagok*, *saudara mara*) already appear in Wilkinson's dictionary. He also corrects the explanation of *bagok* as a proper name, distinguishing it from the English "Reynard" and linking it to specific physical features of both a monkey and a catfish (p. 182).

Turning to Bee's Kelantan place names, Maxwell rejects the derivation of *tumpat* from *ketupat* (a type of cake), pointing to the presence of a definite *m* in the former that is absent in the latter, and insists that Kelantan phonology must be understood before etymological claims are made (p. 183). He further challenges Bee's derivation of *Tabal* from *Tak Bai* ("place of drying sails"), suggesting instead a connection to *tambak* (reclaimed land) or *tambal* (patching). Maxwell closes by recommending that dialect words be verified in Jawi script to reveal sounds unfamiliar to non-local speakers (p. 183).
