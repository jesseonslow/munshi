# Haji Ka-Ta-Na-Ka-La

This short note by C.O. Blagden (1910) corrects a suggestion made by W. George Maxwell regarding the identity of the Javanese ruler "Haji Ka-ta-na-ka-la" as recorded in the *History of the Yuan Dynasty*.

## Summary

Blagden argues that Maxwell's identification of the name as *Haji Kadir Nakhoda* (a ship's captain who had performed the pilgrimage) is incorrect. Citing Groeneveldt's translation of the relevant passage, he demonstrates that "Haji Ka-ta-na-ka-la" was a ruling prince of Java who had been killed by the prince of Kalang, and that the term "Haji" here represents the old Javanese word *haji* meaning "king," not the Malay-Arabic term for a pilgrim (p. 156). Blagden identifies the figure as Kertanagara, the last ruling prince of Tumapel, who was dethroned by Jaya Katong of Daha and whose son-in-law Raden Wijaya went on to found Majapahit. He notes that the Chinese and Javanese accounts are corroborated by contemporary inscriptions (p. 157).
