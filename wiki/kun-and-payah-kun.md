# Kun and payah kun

W.G. Maxwell's short note identifies the etymology of the Malay pawang-lore terms *kun* and *payah kun*, which had previously resisted explanation.

## Summary

Maxwell notes that *kun* is straightforwardly the Arabic "let it be," but the antithetical term *payah kun* had been variously (and unsatisfactorily) explained as a corruption of Malay *supaia* or Arabic *fihi*. Drawing on a suggestion from J.C. Sugars of the F.M.S. Civil Service, Maxwell demonstrates that *payah kun* is a corruption of the Arabic *fa yakun*—"and it is"—making the full expression "Let it be, saith Allah; and it is, saith Muhammad" (p. 25).

Maxwell then traces the phrase to its source in the Koran (2:110 and 36:83) and its parallel in Genesis, and speculates that the sentence was originally coined by *pawangs* as a mantra in the early period of Malay conversion to Islam, with the addition of "saith Muhammad" arising from a combination of ignorance, the national love of antithesis, and the zeal of a convert (p. 26).
