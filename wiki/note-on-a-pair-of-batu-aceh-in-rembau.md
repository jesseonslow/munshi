# Note on a pair of batu aceh in Rembau

Dr. Zakaria Ali's 1995 note documents a pair of Batu Aceh (Acehnese-style Islamic gravestones) in Kampung Cembung Kecil, Rembau, Negeri Sembilan — a find that fills a gap in Othman Yatim's 1988 typological survey, which had not recorded any specimens in the state. Ali argues that the pair, classified as Type C and dated to the 16th century, was likely procured from Johor by Raja Asil for the posthumous burial of his father, Raja Adil, thereby linking Rembau's early political history to the broader Acehnese funerary tradition of the Johor River valley.

## Summary

The article is a site report combining epigraphic description, oral history, and historical inference. Ali provides detailed measurements of nine pairs of granite grave markers arranged on a five-foot mound, of which only one pair (Pair F) exhibits the distinctive Batu Aceh form — a crowned, winged slab with petal motifs, paterae, and floral frames at the base. The absence of inscriptions means dating must rely on typological comparison and contextual evidence rather than direct textual proof.

The historical argument proceeds through a chain of identifications drawn from local memory and colonial records. The Batu Aceh pair marks the grave of "Tok Niko Tandan," a figure of high rank whose identity Ali reconstructs as Raja Adil, a chief of Rembau who appeared as a delegate of the Regent of Johor in a 1757 treaty negotiation at Melaka. Raja Adil's son, Raja Asil, assumed the title "Sultan Muhammad Shah, Yang di-Pertuan Muda of Rembau" in 1798. Ali proposes that Raja Asil purchased the Batu Acehs from Johor — where numerous specimens line the river around Kota Tinggi and Sayung Pinang — for his father's grave, a practice consistent with the prestige economy of early 18th-century Malay polities.

Oral testimony from the site's caretakers, Mansur bin Siput and Maimunah binti Amin, and from the village's oldest resident, Tuan Haji Abdul Hamid bin Alih (aged 107 at the time of fieldwork), situates the tombstones as pre-existing the village's founding in the 1850s. The site functioned as a seasonal pilgrim centre where villagers performed *bayar nazar* (vow-payment) rituals, and local belief held that the stones emitted *barakah* and that a *harimau jadian* (spiritual tiger) named "Kelabu" was raised by Tok Niko Tandan and later inherited by successive *dukun*.

### Key Findings

- The Batu Aceh pair is classified as Type C in Othman Yatim's 14-type system, a form generally dated to the 16th century (p. 83).
- Othman Yatim's 1988 survey *Batu Aceh: Early Islamic Gravestones in Peninsular Malaysia* did not list Negeri Sembilan as a state containing specimens, making this pair a new addition to the known distribution (p. 88, n. 1).
- The headstone measures 38 inches in height with 17-inch wings; the footstone is 39 inches high and 17 inches wide, with a 30-inch inter-stone distance (p. 82).
- Dutch records confirm Rembau was settled by the 1700s; Raja Adil and a Dato' of Rembau negotiated a treaty at Melaka in 1757 as delegates of the Regent of Johor (p. 88).
- Raja Asil assumed the title "Sultan Muhammad Shah, Yang di-Pertuan Muda of Rembau" in 1798 upon his father's death (p. 88).
- The footstone at the Makam Raja Putih in Kota Tinggi closely resembles the Rembau footstone, supporting a Johor provenance (p. 88).

### Conclusion

Ali's definitive takeaway is that the Rembau Batu Acehs represent a material link between the Acehnese funerary tradition of the Johor River valley and the early political formation of Rembau, most plausibly introduced by Raja Asil in the late 18th century as a mark of dynastic prestige for his father Raja Adil. The find corrects the geographic distribution mapped by Yatim and demonstrates that Acehnese-influenced gravestones penetrated further west into the Malay Peninsula than previously documented.

## Context

- The study draws on Othman Yatim's typological corpus, Wilkinson's *Papers on Malay Subjects* for the 1757 treaty record, and Haji Buyong Adil's *Sejarah Negeri Sembilan* for Rembau's dynastic genealogies.
- Historiographically, the note contributes a single-site correction to the known distribution of Batu Aceh and illustrates how oral tradition, when cross-referenced with colonial documents, can recover the identity of otherwise anonymous early modern Malay elites.
