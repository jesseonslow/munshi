# Srivijaya, Yava en Kataha. Tijdschrift voor Indische Taal-, Land– en Volkenkunde (Deel LXXVII Aflevering 3, 1937) uitgeven door het Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschap-pen. An abridged translation R.J. de Touche

E. M. Satow's *Essay Towards a Bibliography of Siam* (1886) is a comprehensive reference work cataloguing published materials relating to the Kingdom of Siam, its languages, and its geography. The portion reproduced here comprises Part II (Periodicals and Proceedings of Learned Societies), Part III (Language), and Part IV (Maps), continuing from an earlier instalment in the same journal. The work serves as a scholarly index for researchers of Siamese history, linguistics, and colonial-era Southeast Asian studies, drawing on French, English, Dutch, and German periodical literature as well as Bangkok-based publications.

## Summary

This bibliography is a systematic catalogue of secondary and tertiary sources on Siam, organised by type rather than chronology. Part II surveys periodical literature from the 1820s through the mid-1880s, covering French journals such as the *Annales de l'Extrême Orient* (1878–84), the *Revue Maritime et Coloniale*, and the *Bulletin de la Société de Géographie*, alongside English publications including the *Asiatic Journal and Monthly Register*, the *Journal of the Indian Archipelago and Eastern Asia* (Logan's journal, 1847–63), and the *Tijdschrift voor Indische Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde*. The entries are annotated with page references and brief descriptions of content, enabling a researcher to locate specific articles on Siamese history, diplomacy, religion, and natural history without consulting each periodical individually.

Part III catalogues grammatical and lexicographical works on the Siamese (Thai) language, spanning from the 1810 *Comparative Vocabulary of the Burma, Malayu and T'hai Languages* printed at Serampore through to the 1886 second edition of the *English and Siamese Vocabulary*. This section tracks the development of Thai linguistic scholarship from missionary and colonial origins—Pallegoix's *Grammatica Linguae Thai* (1850) and *Dictionarium* (1854), Low's *Grammar of the Thai* (1828)—through to the more rigorous comparative-linguistic work of the 1870s and 1880s by Cust, Forbes, Ewald, and Kuhn. Part IV lists maps of Siam and the wider Indochinese region, from a 17th-century Jesuit map through to Hallett's 1886 survey map presented to the Royal Geographical Society.

The work is notable for its inclusion of Bangkok-based English-language periodicals—the *Bangkok Recorder* (1865–67), *Siam Repository* (1869–74), *Siam Times* (from 1864), and *Siam Weekly Advertiser* (1869–76)—which represent a locally produced press that is otherwise poorly represented in Western library collections. Satow's annotations occasionally extend beyond mere identification, noting content such as meteorological observations, shipping returns, and diplomatic events.

### Key Findings

- The *Annales de l'Extrême Orient* (Paris, 1878–84, 6 vols.) is the single most heavily cited periodical, with entries spanning all six volumes covering Siamese inscriptions, the Isthmus of Kra canal, the cremation of the Queen of Siam, and Bangkok shipping returns for 1879 (pp. 163–166).
- The *Journal of the Indian Archipelago and Eastern Asia* (Logan, 1847–63, 9 vols. + New Series 4 vols.) contains foundational articles including Low's *On the Laws of Muang Thai or Siam* (vol. I, pp. 328–426) and an analysis of the Ancient Annals of Siam (vol. II, pp. 568–580) (p. 171).
- The Siamese language section records 20 distinct works (entries 230–249), the earliest being the 1810 *Comparative Vocabulary* printed at the Serampore Mission Press and the latest the 1886 second edition of the *English and Siamese Vocabulary* (pp. 169–171).
- Bangkok-based English-language periodicals documented include the *Bangkok Recorder* (Jan. 1865–Jan. 1867, proprietor D. B. Bradley), the *Siam Times* (from July 1864, proprietor J. H. Chandler), and the *Siam Weekly Advertiser* (1869–76, S. J. Smith) (pp. 168–174).
- The maps section records a 17th-century Jesuit map (*Le Royaume de Siam avec les Royaumes qui luy sont tributaires*, Amsterdam, J. Ottens) alongside a 4-sheet map "constructed from surveys which the Siamese Government had made A.D. 1867 and 1868" (pp. 172–174).
- Satow's own 1885 article "Notes on the Intercourse between Japan & Siam in the 17th century" is listed in the *Transactions of the Asiatic Society of Japan*, vol. XIII (p. 168).

### Conclusion

Satow's bibliography functions as a foundational research tool for the study of Siam in the late Victorian period, consolidating scattered references across European and Anglo-Indian periodicals into a single accessible index. Its value lies not in original historical argument but in the recovery and cross-referencing of materials—particularly Bangkok-produced English-language journalism and French colonial travel literature—that would otherwise be difficult to locate systematically.
