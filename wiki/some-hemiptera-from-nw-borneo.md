# Some Hemiptera from N.W. Borneo

E. Bergroth, a Finnish entomologist based at the Helsingfors Museum, published this taxonomic contribution in 1921 describing thirteen new species of Heteroptera (true bugs) from Sarawak, Borneo, drawn from material collected by J. C. Moulton. The paper extends Bergroth's earlier 1913 treatment of Reduviidae from the same collection, broadening the faunal inventory across five families and establishing several species as new records for Borneo.

## Summary

The article addresses the underdocumented heteropteran fauna of north-western Borneo at a time when the region's entomological knowledge remained fragmentary. Bergroth works through the material family by family—Pentatomidae, Coptosomididae, Coreidae, Myodochidae, and Reduviidae—providing full morphological descriptions for each new species alongside brief locality notes for previously known taxa. The Coreidae receive the most extensive treatment, with four new species in *Homoeocerus* and *Colpura* plus a new species in *Stenocolpura*, reflecting both the diversity of the group in the region and the taxonomic difficulties it presented.

Beyond species descriptions, the paper makes several taxonomic corrections of note. Bergroth unites *Colpura armillata* Breddin with *C. variipes* Westwood on the basis of genital segment structure, proposes the replacement name *Tiarocoris suppositus* for a misapplied *T. luminatus*, and elevates *Stenocolpura* from subgeneric to full generic status, detailing the diagnostic characters that separate it from *Colpura*. He also corrects Montandon's original description of *Spathocrates* regarding head proportions, using a cotype of *S. atroaeneus* to demonstrate the error.

The localities span a range of elevations and habitats across Sarawak, from lowland sites near Kuching (Rock Road, Sadong, Santubong) to montane localities such as Mt. Matang at 3,200 feet and the Marapok Mountains on the border with British North Borneo. Bergroth observes that Coreidae of the division Hygiaria appear more species-rich in mountain regions than in the lowlands, a pattern he notes without extensive elaboration.

### Key Findings

- Thirteen new species are described: *Tolumnia papulifera*, *Aeschrocoris saucius*, *Spathocrates neuter*, *Brachyplatys submarginatus*, *Tiarocoris decoratus*, *Homoeocerus pupillatus*, *H. herbaceus*, *H. breviplicatus*, *Colpura brevipennis*, *C. diplochela*, *Stenocolpura annulata*, *Dasynus shelfordi*, and *Sastrapada oxyptera* (pp. 76–87).
- *Pygolampis foeda* Stål is recorded from Borneo for the first time, extending its known range from Ceylon to Australia and the Fiji Islands; Bergroth also unites *P. biguttata* Reuter as a synonym (p. 86).
- *Colpura armillata* Breddin is synonymised with *C. variipes* Westwood on the basis of consistent genital segment structure across variable specimens from a single locality (p. 83).
- *Stenocolpura* is elevated to full generic rank, distinguished from *Colpura* by head proportions, absence of the postocular callus, evenly rounded male genital segment, and triangularly incised (rather than cleft) sixth female ventral segment (p. 85).
- *Dasynus shelfordi* is named in memory of the late R. Shelford, acknowledged as a major contributor to knowledge of the Bornean fauna (p. 86).
- Types of all new species are deposited in the Helsingfors Museum, with cotypes of a few placed in the Sarawak Museum (p. 76).

### Conclusion

Bergroth's paper substantially advances the heteropteran fauna of Sarawak, adding thirteen new species and multiple first Bornean records while simultaneously resolving several nomenclatural and generic-level questions. The work stands as a foundational reference for the Coreidae and Coptosomididae of the region, and its taxonomic decisions—particularly the elevation of *Stenocolpura* and the synonymy of *C. armillata*—remain points of reference in subsequent revisions of these groups.

## Context

- The primary material derives from J. C. Moulton's collections in Sarawak, with supplementary specimens from the author's own collection (Sumatra) and the Sarawak Museum (Baram, Trusan).
- The paper represents early 20th-century European taxonomic practice in which a single specialist working from deposited material produced both species descriptions and systematic revisions simultaneously, a model that shaped the faunal literature of British Borneo for decades.
