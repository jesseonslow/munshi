# Note on the wild goat of the Malay Peninsula

This page of the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (No. 45, 1906) contains two short notes: one by Henry Norman on a wild goat specimen from Selangor, and one by H. N. Ridley on the feeding habits of the common Tupai.

## Summary

Norman reports the capture of an adult male *Nemorrhoedus sumatrensis* var. *Swettenhami* on the flat sea coast at Batu, Kuala Langat, Selangor, in April 1905, a location some sixteen miles from the nearest high hill (Jugra). He speculates the animal may have been driven from the interior by a rival and notes it has since become tame in captivity (p. 279).

Ridley documents an observation of a common Tupai (*T. ferruginea*) chasing and ultimately catching a bull-frog (*Callula pulchra*) along a garden path, noting that the Tupai attempted to seize the frog by the leg rather than the back, likely due to the sticky defensive secretion the frog exudes when alarmed. This observation supports Ridley's earlier note (No. XXIX, p. 148) that the species is omnivorous despite its classification in the order Insectivora (p. 279).
