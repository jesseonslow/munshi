# Python’s egg

A short natural history note by N. B. Dennys, published in JSBRAS Vol. 9 (1882), comprising four illustrated items on reptiles, a crustacean, and a flowering plant observed in Singapore.

## Summary

The note opens with a discussion of the reticulated python (*Python reticulatus*), correcting the common misidentification of the species as a boa constrictor and noting that all true boas are of American origin (p. 162). Dennys describes the egg, from which the accompanying illustration was made, as part of a mass of approximately one hundred eggs cemented together by a glutinous substance, around which the female coils herself. He records that the female's temperature rises to 75° Fahrenheit and is maintained for 56 days until the young emerge, each about 16 inches long. The specimen was a gift from the Maharaja of Johor to the Raffles Museum (p. 162).

The remaining items are briefer: a description of the flying lizard (*Draco volens*) with a personal anecdote, a note that no lizard species is poisonous, and an observation on spider predation of lizards (pp. 162–163); a previously unfigured Singapore lobster, illustrated at one-third natural size (p. 163); and the flowering banana (*Musa coccinea*), noted as the most brilliantly flowering of the Musaceae, with a coloured plate that predated Madame Hoola van Nooten's *Choice Flowers, Fruits, and Leaves of Java* (p. 163).
