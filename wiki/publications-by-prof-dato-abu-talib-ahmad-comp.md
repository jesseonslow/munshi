# Publications by Prof. Dato Abu Talib Ahmad (comp

This is a late-nineteenth-century administrative report by E.J. Roe, Acting Assistant District Officer, documenting the Orang Asli (referred to as "Sakeis") in the Kuala Lumpur District, originally published in the *Selangor Journal* in 1897 and reprinted in MBRAS's *Selections from the Selangor Journal* (Reprint No. 26).

## Summary

The report provides a census of known Sakei encampments in the Kuala Lumpur district, totalling no more than 125 individuals (including women and children) distributed across the mukims of Petaling, Ulu Klang, and Batu, with specific population breakdowns by location (p. 127). It describes their nomadic habits, their dependence on durian fruit during the season, and the economic arrangements surrounding durian dusuns following Circular 13 of 1895, which introduced government auction sales in place of private dealings that had previously exposed the Sakeis to exploitation by Malay traders (p. 128). The report notes that approximately $800 was paid to Sakeis from auction sales after a government tithe, with additional private contract sales bringing in further sums, and that the annual value of Sakei-claimed dusuns was about $1,700 in 1897 (p. 128).

Roe concludes that the Sakeis are well able to look after themselves, that they show no desire to settle permanently or take up agriculture, and that they closely resemble Malays in general appearance, often making them difficult to distinguish (p. 129).
