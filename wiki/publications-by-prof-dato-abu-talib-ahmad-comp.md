# Publications by Prof. Dato Abu Talib Ahmad (comp

Paul Kratoska compiled this bibliographic tribute to Prof. Dato Abu Talib Ahmad (d. June 2025) for the *Journal of the Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* in 2025, documenting 18 monographs and 69 book chapters, articles, and reviews that together constitute, in Kratoska's assessment, "a major contribution to historical writing in and on Malaysia" (p. 127). The list serves as a posthumous record of a scholar whose career at Universiti Sains Malaysia from 1984 onward drew on training at Tsukuba University and a Monash University doctorate on the Japanese Occupation of Burma.

## Summary

Abu Talib Ahmad's published output clusters around three dominant themes. The first and most sustained is the Japanese Occupation of Malaya and Southeast Asia (1942–45), a field in which he produced his monograph *Malay-Muslims, Islam and The Rising Sun: 1942-45* (MBRAS, 2003) alongside numerous articles examining Japanese policy towards Islam, marriage and divorce among Malay-Muslims in Johor, the social crisis of the Malay community, and the impact on colonial and anti-colonial armies (pp. 128–131). The second theme is museums and cultural heritage in Malaysia, explored in his NUS Press monograph *Museums, History and Culture in Malaysia* (2017) and a series of articles on state museums, the National Museum, the Royal Museum, and the Tun Abdul Razak Memorial as instruments of national memory (pp. 128–130). The third encompasses broader Southeast Asian and Burmese history, including his *Sejarah Myanmar* (DBP, 2000), studies of Burmese neutralism in the Cold War, and the Hsaya San rebellion (pp. 128, 131).

The bibliography also reveals Abu Talib Ahmad's role as editor and collaborator, co-editing volumes with Richard Mason, Tan Liok Ee, Cheah Boon Kheng, and Kratoska himself, and contributing introductions and chapters to institutional histories of USM. Ten of his publications were handled by MBRAS, underscoring the Society's role as a publishing house for Malaysian historical scholarship (p. 127).

### Key Findings

- Abu Talib Ahmad produced 18 monographs and 69 shorter works over a career spanning 1984 to 2024, with his final book *Sejarah Negeri Pahang hingga 1945* still in the indexing stage at the time of his death (p. 127).
- His Japanese Occupation research formed a coherent body of work, including a dedicated MBRAS monograph (2003), a *Journal of Southeast Asian Studies* article reassessing Japanese policy towards Islam in Malaya (2002), and multiple contributions to edited volumes on war and memory (pp. 128, 130–131).
- His museum studies scholarship, culminating in *Museums, History and Culture in Malaysia* (NUS Press, 2017), addressed the political function of state museums in constructing national identity, including the Tun Abdul Razak Memorial and the Royal Museum (pp. 128, 130).
- His Burmese studies, rooted in his Monash doctoral work, included *Sejarah Myanmar* (DBP, 2000), an article on Burma–US relations during the Cold War (1992), and studies of Japanese military administration in Burma drawing on Japanese-language sources (pp. 128, 131–132).
- He contributed encyclopaedia entries to the *Ensiklopedia Sejarah & Kebudayaan Melayu* (DBP) on topics including the Japanese Occupation in Malaya and the *Giyu Gun* and *Giyu Tai* (pp. 131).
- His work on adat law and Malay social stability, published in MBRAS and SEAMEO volumes (2007), represents a distinct strand connecting legal history to questions of social order in the Malay world (p. 130).

### Conclusion

Kratoska's bibliography presents Abu Talib Ahmad as a scholar of unusual breadth whose work bridged Japanese-language source material, Malay-Muslim social history, and the politics of cultural memory in postcolonial Malaysia. The concentration of his output on the Japanese Occupation period and on museums as sites of national narrative construction suggests a sustained intellectual project: understanding how ordinary communities experienced imperial violence and how the postcolonial state subsequently curated that memory.

## Context

- MBRAS handled 10 of Abu Talib Ahmad's publications, including his 2003 monograph on Malay-Muslims during the Japanese Occupation and his 2007 contributions to *New Perspectives and Directions in Malaysian Historical Research* (p. 127).
- The bibliography was published in the same issue of *JMBRAS* that carried his obituary, reflecting the Society's practice of commemorating deceased members through their published legacy (p. 127).
