# A forgotten naval battle

K. G. Tregonning's short note recovers the account of a naval engagement fought in the Macassar Strait on 26 July 1806, discovered in an early issue of the *Prince of Wales Island Gazette* and absent from standard naval histories such as Parkinson's *War in the Eastern Seas* (p. 202).

The action involved H.M.S. *Greyhound* (Captain Elphinstone) and H.M.S. *Harrier* (Captain Troubridge), part of Admiral Sir Thomas Troubridge's fleet based at Penang, which encountered a Dutch squadron of four vessels. Employing a raking tactic that Tregonning notes as a "Nelsonian touch," the two British ships subjected the leading Dutch frigate to fire from both sides, resulting in the capture of the frigate *Pallas* (36 guns), the armed ship *Victoria* (18 guns), and the *Batavia* (10 guns), while the corvette *William* escaped (p. 203). British losses were minimal: one seaman killed and six wounded. The captured *Pallas* was commissioned as H.M.S. *Macassar* under Captain Troubridge, the admiral's son (p. 203). Tregonning closes by noting that the despatches left Penang on the *Blenheim*, which foundered off Madagascar, likely consigning the prize-money and official reports to obscurity (p. 204).
