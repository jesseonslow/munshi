# Notes on the meanings of some Malay words

J.A. Baker's short note proposes corrections and additions to definitions in Wilkinson's *Malay-English Dictionary* (2nd ed., 1932), based on his own usage observations and corroborating evidence from other lexicographic sources.

## Summary

Baker examines seven Malay words—*antah*, *jelantah*, *bernas*, *api-api*, *sidang*, *harus*, and *sědang*—arguing that Wilkinson's definitions are imperfect or incomplete. The most detailed entry concerns *antah*, which Baker redefines as "grains of unhusked *padi* which remain in the *beras* after pounding," supported by a proverb, native informants, and cross-references to Favre, Van Ronkel, and Winstedt (pp. 150–151). For *jelantah* he adds two senses (hard grains in cooked rice; empty grains in *padi*), and for *bernas* he notes a Negri Sembilan usage meaning "well-filled grains" (p. 151). He also identifies what he considers a printer's error in Wilkinson's entry for *api-api* (conflating Avicennia with Loranthaceae), corrects *sidang* to mean "to stop raining" rather than "settling down to a downpour," adds the colloquial sense "probably" for *harus*, and extends the definition of *sědang* beyond "although" to cover concessive uses where that translation fails (pp. 151–152). Baker explicitly invites readers to confirm or dispute the meanings proposed.
