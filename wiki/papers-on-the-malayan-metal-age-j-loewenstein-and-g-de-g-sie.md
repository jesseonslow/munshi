# Papers on the Malayan metal age. J. Loewenstein and G. de G. Sieveking. Monographs on Malay subjects, No. 3

"A Rice-Ceremony" is a brief note by R. O. Winstedt describing a rice-planting mock combat observed at Johol, published in the Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, No. 77 (1917).

## Summary

Winstedt reports having witnessed the *bapua* (or *singketa*) ceremony at Johol, a mock combat in which two parties hurled *puar* rods at one another across a gully to expel evil influences from the rice-fields before planting. He notes the rods were approximately 3½ feet long, thin, green, and straight, with a flat pared-root end resembling a stethoscope, and suggests they may be stems of a ginger-plant. A *pawang* opened the proceedings with an invocation, and the combat lasted about half an hour until one participant received a bloody blow to the face (p. 249).

The note references earlier accounts by C. O. Blagden (J. R. A. S., 1896) and W. G. Skeat (*Malay Magic*, p. 250), who recorded the ceremony in Minangkabau and Selangor respectively, and adds that *singketa* is a more serious variant using heavier weapons (*batang pisang*) (p. 249).
