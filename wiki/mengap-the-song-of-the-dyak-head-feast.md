# Mengap, the song of the Dyak head feast

A. M. S. (1878) provides a brief geographical note on recent exploratory journeys in four regions of the Malay Peninsula—Ulu Johor, Ulu Kinta, Jelei, and Rambau—published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*.

## Summary

The note reports on several recent ascents and traverses that have expanded knowledge of the Peninsula's physical geography. Gunong Blumut (3,200 ft) in Johor was climbed for the first time by two independent parties, and a range in the interior of Perak was found to include a peak of approximately 8,000 feet, named Mount Robinson by its discoverers (p. 222). The author then raises a central geographical question: whether an important stream exists in the very centre of the Peninsula, draining the eastern slopes of these high mountains to join the Jelei and form the Pahang River together with the Bera (p. 222). Evidence supporting this hypothesis is traced through Mr. Daly's 1875 journey and Baron Verboch's crossing from Sungei Ujong into Jelebu via the River Triang (p. 223).

The note concludes by reproducing a letter from the *Overland Summary* (October 1873) that challenges premature claims about the "true backbone" of the Peninsula, arguing that no one had yet penetrated far enough into the mountain ranges to obtain a view of the China Sea (p. 224). An exploring party under Mr. Leech is noted as about to depart from Kwala Kangsa to address these outstanding questions (p. 225).
