# Katydids

The Malayan katydid fauna, comprising the families Gryllacridae and Tettigoniidae, was comprehensively revised in 1923 by H. H. Karny, a German entomologist based at the Zoological Museum in Buitenzorg, Dutch East Indies, who drew on specimens held at the Raffles Museum, Singapore [[1, pp. 116–193]](#ref-1). His work established a definitive catalogue for the Malayan sub-region, expanding the known fauna from 320 to 357 species and describing 35 new species and one new genus [[1, p. 116]](#ref-1). The revision covered both the Malay Peninsula and Borneo, with 21 species new to the former and four new to the latter [[1, p. 116]](#ref-1).

### Taxonomic Scope and Nomenclatural Revisions

Karny's monograph addressed the full range of subfamilies represented in the region, including Gryllacrinae, Phaneropterinae, Mecopodinae, Phyllophorinae, Pseudophyllinae, Listroscelinae, Conocephalinae, Agraeicinae, and Copiphorinae [[1, p. 116]](#ref-1). A significant proportion of the material examined was taxonomically novel: of the species represented in the Raffles Museum collection, 38 per cent were new to science, and the total number of species known from Malaysia was increased by 11½ per cent [[1, p. 117]](#ref-1). Karny also undertook nomenclatural corrections, most notably separating *Gryllacris signatifrons* De Haan from *G. signatifrons* Serville on the basis of differences in forehead colouration, and proposing the new name *Gryllacris dehaani* for the former [[1, p. 118]](#ref-1). The new genus *Zatricaprion* was erected for a single species, *Z. reticulatus*, distinguished from allied genera by its distinctive tegminal venation and the shape of the male subgenital plate [[1, pp. 171–173]](#ref-1).

### Notable Species and Morphological Diversity

Among the 35 new species described, several stand out for their size or morphological distinctiveness. *Eumegalodon vaginatus* n. sp., with a body length of 51.5 mm and an ovipositor measuring 64 mm—longer than the entire body—represents the first record of the genus from the Malay Peninsula [[1, pp. 186–190]](#ref-1). *Phaula gigantea* n. sp., with tegmina 52 mm long and 13.5 mm wide, is one of the largest Phaneropterinae known from the region; Karny noted it might warrant erection as a new genus [[1, pp. 146–147]](#ref-1). *Xiphidiopsis mirabilis* n. sp. possesses a "very remarkable second plate" of unique shape, which Karny described as having no parallel in any other species hitherto known [[1, p. 180]](#ref-1). *Gryllacris griffinii* n. sp., named in honour of the Italian entomologist Achille Griffini, is a small (20 mm body length) but strikingly coloured species with black-marked pronotum and legs, belonging to the *podocausta*-group [[1, pp. 130–131]](#ref-1).

### Ecological and Economic Significance

The records attest to the ecological prevalence of certain species. *Mecopoda elongata* is documented in six colour morphs across the Raffles Museum collection, ranging from unicolorous green to fuscous with large black blotches, and is explicitly noted as noxious in the region, with references to agricultural literature on sugarcane pests [[1, pp. 160–162]](#ref-1). Similarly, *Pseudophyllus prasinus* is recorded as noxious in Java [[1, p. 163]](#ref-1). The wide distribution of *Mecopoda elongata*—spanning British India, Ceylon, Burma, the Malay Peninsula, China, Japan, the Philippine Islands, Borneo, Java, Sumatra, and Australia—underscores its regional importance [[1, p. 162]](#ref-1).

## Research and Documentation

The Society's literature on Malayan katydids is anchored in Karny's 1923 monograph, published in the inaugural volume of the *Journal of the Malayan Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*. This work forms part of his broader programme of revising the orthopteran fauna of the Dutch East Indies and neighbouring regions, of which his 1920 *Zoologische Mededelingen* paper on Malayan Orthoptera is the immediate precursor [[1, p. 116]](#ref-1). The primary archival source was the Raffles Museum, Singapore, with comparative material drawn from the Buitenzorg Museum [[1, pp. 116–193]](#ref-1). The paper represents a foundational contribution to Malayan entomology, establishing the taxonomic baseline against which subsequent regional studies would be measured. No further articles on Malayan katydids appear in the Society's records for the period covered by this review.
