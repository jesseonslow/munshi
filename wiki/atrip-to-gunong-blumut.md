# Atrip to Gunong Blumut

This is a brief note by Haji Abdul Majid bin Haji Zainuddin describing an anonymous gift-giving custom in Kuala Kangsar, published in the Miscellaneous Notes section of the *Journal of the Malayan Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (1925, pp. 85–86).

## Summary

The note describes a custom in which presents are sent anonymously to a family, accompanied by a poem in *sha'ir* or *pantun* form praising the recipients and recounting their good deeds to the village (p. 86). It is considered a disgrace if the receiving family cannot identify the sender, as courtesy obliges them to give a return gift. However, if the senders are captured before reaching home, they are humiliated by having their faces smeared with soot and being made to eat their own presents in front of the receiving family, which removes the obligation for a return gift (p. 86). The custom thus creates a cycle of reciprocal anonymous gifting, with each party attempting to outwit the other; successfully delivering presents without being caught is regarded as a victory over the opposing party (p. 86).
