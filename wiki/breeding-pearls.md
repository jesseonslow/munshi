# Breeding pearls

This is a short note by Tan Yeok Seong (South Seas Society, Singapore) describing the bronze incense burner from the Sambas hoard of Hindu religious objects discovered in West Borneo, published in the *Journal of the Malayan Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*, Vol. XXII, Pt. IV (1949).

## Summary

The article provides a detailed physical description of the incense burner, which has a rectangular base, a two-storeyed waggon-roof cover, four turtle feet, and is decorated with standing cocks at the corners and sides of the roof, rows of triangular perforations, and a row of impressed pillars with three-quarter circle arches around the walls (p. 19). Tan Yeok Seong situates the vessel within a broader comparative framework, noting that the triangular-perforation design has Indian affinities (citing a Taxila casket), the standing-bird motif appears on Chinese Han-period bronzes from Honan, and the turtle feet suggest the Chinese *Pi-hsai* (p. 20–21). He also notes that the pillar-and-arch design is rare in East Asia and comparable to art from lands west of India (p. 22).

The author concludes that the vessel's multiple stylistic influences make it of exceptional value for historical research on Indonesian culture, and he cites opinions from Dr. Quaritch Wales (who compared the roof to one found in Kedah) and Mr. Basil Gray of the British Museum (who dated the Sambas figures to the 8th–10th century A.D. but was inclined to date the incense casket considerably later) (p. 20).
