# Tom {Harrisson} and the Survival Service Commission

This is a short memorial note by R.S.R. Fitter, a former colleague of Tom Harrisson from Mass Observation, tracing Harrisson's role in the IUCN Survival Service Commission (SSC) from 1963 until his death (p. 71).

## Summary

Fitter outlines how Harrisson's appointment to the SSC in 1963 opened a new phase in his conservation work, building on his earlier Sarawak turtle conservation scheme and his writings on orang-utans. He served as Chairman of the SSC's Orang-utan Specialist Group, where he and his wife Barbara devised the Orang-utan Recovery Scheme (OURS) to rehabilitate illegally imported animals seized by customs authorities, and as first vice-chairman and later co-chairman of the Marine Turtle Group (p. 71).

Fitter identifies Harrisson's 1969 visit to the Philippines with Charles Lindbergh as perhaps his most notable single achievement in the region: the pair established a locally based, officially supported conservation structure for the tamaraw and the monkey-eating eagle that remained intact at the time of Harrisson's death (p. 71). The note closes with a personal tribute to Harrisson's presence on the SSC's Alert Group (later Steering Committee) (p. 72).
