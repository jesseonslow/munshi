# Pteridophyta

## Scope and Historical Definition

The pteridophyte flora of the Mentawai Islands, comprising the islands of Siberut and Sipora off the west coast of Sumatra, was documented in a 1928 taxonomic treatment by R.E. Holttum, based on material collected during a 1924 expedition led by C.B. Kloss. The collection yielded 77 species in total, of which 59 were recorded from Siberut and 29 from Sipora [[1, p. 14]](#ref-1). Holttum noted that the majority of these species are of wide distribution in the Malaysian area, and that the collection probably does not represent a very complete record of the pteridophyte flora, certain universal species such as *Gleichenia linearis* being absent [[1, p. 14]](#ref-1). The nomenclature employed was that of Christensen's *Index Filicum*, and Holttum acknowledged his inability to identify some of the *Selaginella* specimens [[1, p. 14]](#ref-1).

The collection spanned the orders Psilotales, Lycopodiales, and Filicales, the latter encompassing the families Ophioglossaceae, Marattiaceae, Schizaeaceae, Gleicheniaceae, Hymenophyllaceae, Cyatheaaceae, and Polypodiaceae [[1, pp. 14–22]](#ref-1). Habitats recorded included jungle interiors, jungle edges, tree trunks, rotten bark, and streamside locations, with several species noted as epiphytes [[1, pp. 14–22]](#ref-1).

## Taxonomic Contributions and Biogeographic Significance

Two new species were described from the collection. *Alsophila parvifolia*, a small tree fern from Siberut and Sipora, is noted as the only recorded example of a simply pinnate *Alsophila* from Malaysia, comparable in size with *A. phegopteroides* Hooker from Peru [[1, p. 18]](#ref-1). *Polypodium papilliferum*, from Sipora, is distinguished by very narrow ciliate scales and very acute soral papillae [[1, p. 21]](#ref-1).

The collection carried considerable biogeographic weight. *Lygodium borneense* van Alderwerelt van Rosenburgh, previously known only from Borneo, was extended to the Mentawai Islands, and Holttum demonstrated that the previously recognised *forma samarindae* is not a discrete taxon but part of a continuous variation series in secondary petiole branching [[1, pp. 15–16]](#ref-1). He observed that the species appears common in Borneo and remarked that it is remarkable it had not hitherto been collected in the lowlands of Sumatra or the Malay Peninsula, suggesting its smaller forms might have been mistaken for *L. cinnatum* [[1, p. 16]](#ref-1). Similarly, the known range of *Trichomanes aphlebioides* Christ, first described from New Guinea, was considerably increased by the Mentawai material and a specimen from Gunong Panti, Johore (Ridley 4147) in the Singapore herbarium [[1, p. 17]](#ref-1). Holttum described the aphlebiae as very well developed, originating in the axils of normal fronds, copiously branched, and reaching 11 cm in length, with distinctly winged main branches [[1, p. 17]](#ref-1).

Further taxonomic clarification was offered for *Microlepia Ridleyi* Copeland, confirmed from Sipora. Holttum argued that older Peninsula collections identified as *M. Kurzii* Clarke should be reinterpreted as a hairy form of *M. platyphylla* (Don), and that *M. puberula* van Alderwerelt van Rosenburgh from Java may be identical to *M. Ridleyi* [[1, p. 21]](#ref-1).

## Research and Documentation

The paper forms part of the broader "Spolia Mentawiensia" series, a systematic botanical survey of the Mentawai Islands conducted during the colonial period [[1]](#ref-1). Type material is deposited in the Raffles Museum, Singapore, and comparative material was examined at the Buitenzorg Herbarium and Kew [[1, pp. 14]](#ref-1). The work exemplifies the taxonomic methodology of the period: careful morphological description, range extension, and the resolution of nomenclatural confusion through direct comparison of herbarium specimens across multiple institutions. Holttum's treatment remains a foundational reference for the pteridophyte flora of the Mentawai Islands.
