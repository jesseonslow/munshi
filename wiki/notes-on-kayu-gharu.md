# Notes on kayu gharu

R.N. Bland's "Notes on Kayu Gharu" (JSBRAS Vol. 18, 1886, pp. 359–361) documents the ethnobotanical and commercial aspects of agarwood (*kayu gharu*) as gathered from Malay and *Pawang* informants in Ulu Muar and Johol.

## Summary

The note describes the *tabak* tree (source of *gharu*), the process by which the *Pawang* locates and harvests the diseased heart-wood, and the associated ritual formulae and taboos (p. 359–360). Bland records eight diagnostic features for identifying *gharu*-bearing trees in the standing state, and classifies eight varieties of the wood—*chandan*, *tandok*, *menjulong-ulong*, *sikat*, *sikat lampam*, *bulu rusa*, *kemandangan*, and *wangkang*—distinguished by density, colour, texture, and buoyancy in water (p. 361). The *chandan* and *tandok* are noted as the most valuable, priced between $200 and $50 per *pikul*, and used in incense and aromatic oil production by both Chinese and Malay communities (p. 361).
