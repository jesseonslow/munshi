# Words of majesty: a brief history of royal correspondence between England and Asia, 1600–1858

## Abstract

This paper looks at ways in which Raffles’ membership of Dutch masonic lodges proved beneficial to his personal initiatives and professional career both in Java (1811–16) and subsequently as founder of Singapore (1819). Contradictory statements abound about the date of his admission to the Masonic Order. The dominant view is that he was initiated in the Lodge Virtutis et Artis Amici in 1813, a view based on a certificate dated to the masonic year 5813, which says that ‘T. Stamford Raffles’ had received the first two masonic degrees of Entered Apprentice and Fellow of the Craft in that lodge. Scrutiny of the certificate on the basis of a new photographic image, however, shows that the document itself and plans for the foundation of the lodge predate the British interregnum. At the same time, current interpretations of the masonic dating cannot be reconciled with Raffles’ elevation to the degree of Master Mason in the Lodge De Vriendschap in Surabaya on 5 July 1813. Establishing the correct date of Raffles’ initiation in September–October 1811 is therefore essential both for the reconstruction of his masonic career in Java, and an understanding of the special supranational character of the British interregnum itself.

## Summary

This article addresses a persistent chronological confusion in Dutch and British masonic historiography regarding the date of Thomas Stamford Raffles' initiation into Freemasonry during the British interregnum in Java (1811–16). The dominant Dutch view, established in 1931 and reiterated in subsequent scholarship, places his admission in 1813 at the Lodge Virtutis et Artis Amici, a lodge supposedly founded specifically for his initiation. Jordaan and Carey demonstrate through close philological analysis of the initiation certificate—reproduced here for the first time in a new photographic image—that this dating is irreconcilable with the confirmed date of Raffles' elevation to Master Mason in Surabaya on 5 July 1813, which cannot logically have preceded his initiation. They establish that the initiation most probably took place between 11 September and 19 October 1811, during Lord Minto's brief presence in Java, and that the certificate paper itself predates the British occupation (pp. 6–7, 11).

The authors argue that correcting this date is not merely a matter of masonic record-keeping but is essential for understanding the British interregnum as a form of "supranational masonic government." The simultaneous initiation of Raffles and his Dutch Council colleague Harman Warner Muntinghe at the same ceremony, in the presence of Lord Minto and Colonel Gillespie, created a bond that underpinned Anglo-Dutch administrative co-operation across law, land tenure, archaeology, and social institutions. The article draws on Dutch masonic archives at the Cultureel Maconniek Centrum in The Hague, British masonic periodicals (*The Pentagon*), and Raffles' own correspondence to reconstruct this network and its implications for the governance of Java (pp. 12–17).

### Key Findings

- The initiation certificate for Lodge Virtutis et Artis Amici bears the masonic date "26th day of the 6th month, Year of Light 5813," normally equated with 26 August 1813, but the paper itself predates the British interregnum, as evidenced by the name of Pieter Brouwer (Grand Secretary, 1801–1810) in the margin and a crossed-out reference to the Batavian Republic (pp. 6–7).
- Raffles' elevation to Master Mason in the Lodge De Vriendschap, Surabaya, is confirmed by lodge minutes as having taken place on 5 July 1813, a date independently corroborated by British Mason B.N. Powell's translation of those minutes published in *The Pentagon* (1928) (pp. 5, 13–14).
- Lord Minto departed Java aboard H.M.S. *Modeste* on 19 October 1811, fixing the latest possible date for Raffles' initiation; his second Proclamation announcing Raffles' appointment as Lieutenant-Governor on 11 September 1811 provides the earliest plausible date (p. 11).
- Raffles paid 75 Spanish Dollars (approximately £1,050 in present-day purchasing power) in April 1815 for the degree of Scots Master, and 150 Spanish Dollars (approximately £2,100) in March 1816 for the degree of Sovereign Prince of the Rose Croix, as recorded in the financial records of the Chapter of La Vertueuse (p. 17).
- Of 59 Dutch jury members appointed in Batavia in April 1812, 30 could be identified as Masons; of 78 newly registered members of the Batavian Literary Society, at least 43 were Masons (pp. 28, 32).
- The Lodge La Vertueuse in Batavia counted 20 British members out of 61 total members by early 1814, including Raffles (p. 13).

### Conclusion

Jordaan and Carey conclude that the British interregnum in Java was underpinned by a dense network of Anglo-Dutch masonic relationships that functioned as the institutional backbone of a unique supranational administrative experiment. The simultaneous initiation of Raffles and Muntinghe, and the pervasive masonic composition of the Java Council and its subordinate bodies, reveal that Freemasonry was not a peripheral social club but a crucial mechanism of political co-operation and personnel selection during 1811–16.

## Context

- **Primary archival collections:** Cultureel Maconniek Centrum (CMC), The Hague (lodge minutes, financial records, membership lists, certificates); *The Pentagon* (British masonic periodical, 1928–1931 issues); Raffles-Minto Collection, India Office Library.
- **Historiographical contribution:** Corrects a dating error that has persisted in Dutch masonic historiography since 1931 and repositions Freemasonry from a marginal social interest to a central structural element in the governance of the British interregnum.
