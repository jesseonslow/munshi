# A reputed Acehnese sarakata of the Jamal al-Lail dynasty

Daniel Crecelius and E.A. Beardow published this article in 1979, examining a *sarakata* (royal decree) attributed to the mid-nineteenth-century sultanate of Aceh that purports to confirm the property rights of the Jamal al-Lail dynasty. Their central thesis is that the document is a late nineteenth-century forgery, produced by a figure known as Teungku di Mule' to legitimize a land claim, and they support this by identifying numerous internal inconsistencies, anachronisms, and genealogical fabrications.

## Summary

The article presents a full transliteration and translation of a *sarakata* originally dated 1266 A.H. (1849) and copied in 1289 A.H. (1872), which claims to confirm that certain lands in the three *mukims* of Kayee Adang were the private property of Sultan Badr al-'Alam Sharif Hashim (r. 1699–1702), founder of the Jamal al-Lail dynasty, and that these lands had devolved to four named inheritors descended from Sultan Badr al-Munir. The document was written in a mixture of Arabic, Malay, and Acehnese, and bears the seal of Sultan 'Ala' al-Din Mahmud Shah, though the authors note the seal is illegible in the photocopy they worked from.

Crecelius and Beardow situate the *sarakata* within a broader group of several dozen manuscripts attributed to a scribal figure called Teungku di Mule' (Sayyid 'Abdallah ibn Ahmad ibn 'Ali Al Jamal al-Lail), who styled himself 'abd al-miskin al-faqir al-gharib. Building on Henri Chambert-Loir's 1976 field report, which had already raised serious doubts about the authenticity of these documents, the authors extend his classification by identifying a distinct "property inheritance" category within the corpus. They argue that the *sarakata* under examination shares the hallmarks of the broader group: it names a previously unreported Sultan (Badr al-'Alam Zain al-'Abidin) inserted into the Jamal al-Lail genealogy; it references a 21-minister bureaucracy and foreign administrative terms (*mayor*, *sekreteris*) that are implausible for mid-nineteenth-century Aceh; and it contains a list of 74 named individuals purported to be peasants of Sultan Badr al-'Alam Sharif Hashim in the early eighteenth century—a list that reappears, with minor variations, in another document from the same group as the members of a seventeenth-century parliament.

The authors conclude that no evidence exists outside this di Mule' series for either a seventeenth-century Acehnese parliament or a nineteenth-century bureaucracy of 21 ministries, and that the precise identification of 74 individuals from a period a century and a half earlier, often by first name alone, is itself a mark of fabrication. The document's purpose, they argue, was to provide a genealogical and legal basis for Sayyid 'Abdallah's claim to Jamal al-Lail family property, including a separate *sarakata/waqfiyah* that granted him exclusive ownership of Badr al-Munir's fort in Gampong Jawa.

### Key Findings

- The *sarakata* is dated 22 Muharram 1266 A.H. (8 December 1849) as the original and was copied in 1289 A.H. (1872); it purports to have been issued under Sultan 'Ala' al-Din Shah Jahan (Ibrahim, r. 1857–1870) and validated during the reign of his successor 'Ala' al-Din Mahmud Shah (r. 1870–1874) (pp. 55, 62–63).
- The document names 74 individuals as "subjects" of Sultan Badr al-'Alam Sharif Hashim (r. 1699–1702), listing their names in a fixed sequence; the same list reappears in another di Mule' document as the membership of a parliament during the reign of Safiyat al-Din (1641–1675) (pp. 55–56).
- A previously unknown Sultan, Badr al-'Alam Zain al-'Abidin Al Jamal al-Lail, is inserted into the genealogy as having ruled for seven months in 1111 A.H. (1699), a figure attested only in this group of documents (pp. 53–54).
- One related *sarakata* in the authors' possession lists a government of 21 ministers, including a Minister of Agriculture, a Minister of Education, a Minister of Communications, and a Minister of Public Works—functionaries for which no independent evidence exists in nineteenth-century Aceh (p. 55).
- The property at issue comprises the *huma* and *blang* of Sikeumeu, open fields for military drill, and firewood forest in the three *mukims* of Kayee Adang (Lam Kruet Lambaro, Lambaro, and Lheue), bounded by the Aceh River and paths to Berok, Lamprik, Tunggul, and Theun Taleu (pp. 63–65).
- Chambert-Loir's 1976 mission to Aceh identified the Teungku di Mule' manuscripts (dated c. 1840–c. 1880) as forgeries composed in the preceding 25 years, a judgment the authors consider "borne out by numerous inconsistencies" in the property inheritance subset (pp. 54–55).

### Conclusion

Crecelius and Beardow definitively conclude that the *sarakata* must have its authenticity subjected to careful scrutiny before any of its claims can be accepted, and that the weight of internal evidence—fabricated genealogies, anachronistic bureaucratic structures, recycled name-lists, and the self-interested position of the copyist—strongly indicates it is a product of the late nineteenth century designed to support a property claim by the Jamal al-Lail family.

## Context

- The primary source is a photocopy of a slightly damaged manuscript held in the private collection of Dr. Aly Hasjmy, then Rector of IAIN ar-Raniry, Banda Aceh; the authors also examined additional Teungku di Mule' documents in their own possession (p. 49, n. 1).
- The study extends Chambert-Loir's 1976 classification of the di Mule' corpus by identifying a "property inheritance" category and provides the first detailed philological and historical analysis of one such document, contributing to the broader historiographical problem of document authenticity in Acehnese traditional sources.
