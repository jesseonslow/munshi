# The installation of Tuanku Abdul-Rahman ibini Al-Marhum Tuanku Muhammad Shah as Yang di-Pertuan, Negri Sembilan

J.J. Sheehan, a British colonial administrator in Negri Sembilan, published this detailed account in 1936 of the 1934 installation of Tuanku Abdul-Rahman as Yang di-Pertuan Besar, using the 1898 installation of his father Tuanku Muhammad Shah as a comparative baseline to document both continuity and change in court ceremonial. The article serves as a primary-source record of the ritual sequence, the roles of the four Undang, and the material culture of the Sri Menanti court at the height of the colonial period.

## Summary

Sheehan's account is structured around two principal ceremonial acts: the *bersiram* (lustration) performed on the morning of 25 April 1934, and the *tabal* (installation proper) in the afternoon. He frames the entire narrative against E.W. Birch's 1898 account and Wilkinson's *Sri Menanti*, noting where the 1934 proceedings departed from or confirmed earlier practice. The *bersiram*, which Birch and Wilkinson did not mention, is described as an essential preliminary in which the royal retainers and the Penghulu Tanah Mengandong perform a ritual involving the sevenfold circuit of a silver tray and bowl containing *bedak limau* (powder and lime), followed by an Arabic prayer from the Kathi and a lengthy chant by the *ulama*. The *tabal* itself is the more elaborate sequence: the four Undang process separately from the Pintu Gerbang to the *Balai Penghadapan*, the Dato' Klana delivers the formal proclamation, the Bentara Kanan makes the heraldic announcement in his prescribed one-legged posture, and each Undang and chief in turn performs a precisely counted number of obeisances before the singgasana.

The article's historiographical value lies in its granular attention to protocol: the exact number of obeisances (seven ascending, five descending for the Undang; nine ascending, seven descending for the Dato' Shahbandar and territorial chiefs), the order of procession, the specific titles and positions of every participant, and the textual content of the prayers and invocations. Sheehan also records practical departures from custom—such as the Undang lodging at the Istana Lama rather than in separate *balai*, the use of a loudspeaker to amplify the ceremony, and the failure of the *pawang* to prevent a thunderstorm that dispersed the outdoor crowd. The piece closes with the conferral of the K.C.M.G. by the High Commissioner the following morning, marking the intersection of indigenous and British ceremonial authority.

### Key Findings

- The installation took place on Wednesday, 25 April 1934, at the new *balai penghadapan* at Sri Menanti; approximately 10,000 Malays had gathered by 9 a.m. (p. 231).
- The Regalia displayed during *mendirikan adat* comprised 16 yellow-cloth umbrellas, 8 kris, 8 swords, 8 tufted spears, and 16 flags of State (p. 231).
- The four Undang processed in the order: Rembau, Johol, Jelebu, Sungai Ujong, preceded by a 15-gun salute (p. 236).
- The Dato' Klana's proclamation recorded that Tuanku Abdul-Rahman had been designated successor on Thursday, 3 August 1933, three days before his father's death (p. 237).
- The *bersiram* ceremony involved the Orang Empat Istana carrying the silver tray and bowl seven times around the dais before presenting *bedak limau* to the Yang di-Pertuan and Tengku Ampuan (p. 233).
- A Guard of Honour of 100 M.V.I. under three officers was posted on the lawn facing the Pintu Gerbang (p. 233).

### Conclusion

Sheehan's definitive takeaway is that the 1934 installation confirmed the remarkable tenacity of Negri Sembilan court custom over a thirty-six-year span, with only minor adaptations necessitated by modern infrastructure and transport. The ritual architecture—its prescribed obeisances, its heraldic postures, its prayer texts—remained essentially unchanged from 1898, demonstrating that the constitutional relationship between the Yang di-Pertuan Besar and the four Undang, codified in the 1898 Treaty, continued to function as the living framework of the state's political order.

## Context

- The author, J.J. Sheehan (M.C.S.), was a British colonial officer in Negri Sembilan; his account is written from the perspective of an administrative insider with direct access to court proceedings and the Private Secretary's planning documents.
- The article draws on Birch's 1898 account (JMBRAS Vol. 46) and Wilkinson's *Sri Menanti* as comparative sources, and includes full Malay-language texts of the proclamation and the Arabic prayer as an appendix, making it a key primary source for the study of Negri Sembilan court ceremonial in the interwar period.
