# Flying lizard

This is a brief natural history note by N. B. Dennys, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (Vol. 9, 1882), describing the flying lizard (*Draco volens*) as found in Singapore (p. 162).

## Summary

Dennys provides a short account of *Draco volens*, noting that it abounds in Singapore and is capable of a considerable length of flight. He describes the eggs as tiny, about the size of peas, and remarks that he never succeeded in obtaining an embryo or seeing a newly-hatched specimen (p. 162).

He adds a general observation that no species of lizard is in any sense poisonous and that very few possess teeth developed enough to inflict a wound, and notes that spiders are great enemies of lizards, typically catching them asleep and weaving a web around the mouth before biting the lip (p. 163). The note closes with an invitation for readers to keep the animal in confinement and furnish further information.
