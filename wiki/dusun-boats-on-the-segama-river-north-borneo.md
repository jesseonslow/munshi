# Dusun boats on the Segama River, North Borneo

F. H. Fitch's short note describes the construction and use of the *alud*, a Dusun dug-out boat on the Segama River in North Borneo, with particular attention to its integral seat and the temporary bark superstructure fitted before descending rapids.

## Summary

The note documents the *konkod*, a permanent seat carved as an integral part of the dug-out hull at roughly three-foot intervals, with bamboo slats and split rattan lashed across the carved supports (p. 211). The slots at the back of each support serve multiple purposes: lashing down cargo, accepting upright saplings for a temporary *kajang* sun-roof, and providing sockets for the sapling framework used to raise the freeboard.

Fitch then gives a detailed account of fitting the *tambir* (Dusun) or *dinding* (Malay)—a bark superstructure lashed to a sapling framework (*gagalang*) before shooting the severe upper Segama rapids. The bark is taken from *majau* or *lampong* trees, identified by H. G. Keith as *Shorea leptoclados*, *Pentace* sp., *Pterospermum* spp., or possibly *Dryobalanops* sp. (p. 211). The lower edges are pegged with *melindok* wood or bamboo, the joins caulked with *tadok* (twisted green bark from *litak* sticks), and the overlapping strips at bows and stern carved into a decorative *warong* shape. Fitch notes the practice is widespread on East Coast rivers and Kinabatangan tributaries, with material quality varying from bark to palm leaves, and on the Sembakong River the *tambir* are made of soft wood as a more permanent feature (p. 212).
