# The comparative philology of the Sakai and Semang dialects of the Malay Peninsula – a review

C. O. Blagden's brief note, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (1902), is an appeal to members of the Society for unpublished dialect material relating to the aboriginal peoples of the Malay Peninsula.

## Summary

Blagden announces that he is engaged in collecting and comparing the dialects of the "Wild Tribes" of the Peninsula and requests contributions of vocabularies, grammatical notes, sentence specimens with verbatim translations, and lists of personal names, each accompanied by the tribe's name, location, and physical characteristics (p. 141). He notes that much valuable material remains unpublished because collectors lack the time to complete their work, and expresses particular need for specimens from Negri Sembilan and Pahang (p. 141).

The note also draws attention to the neglect of local Malay dialects, arguing that they are of great philological importance rather than mere corruptions of the standard language, and contrasts the Society's progress unfavourably with Dutch recording efforts in the Archipelago (p. 142). Blagden specifies the kinds of data needed—intonation and accent, pronunciation of particular syllables, and grammatical or syntactical peculiarities—and adds that place-names, especially those without Malay etymology, may illuminate the pre-Malay condition of the Peninsula (pp. 142–143).
