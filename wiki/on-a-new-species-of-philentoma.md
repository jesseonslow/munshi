# On a new species of “Philentoma”

E. Bartlett's 1895 note, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (Vol. 28, pp. 96–97), described a new species of *Philentoma* (a genus of Old World flycatchers) from Borneo. The paper is a brief two-page description, and its taxonomic subject was subsequently synonymized: the species Bartlett erected was shown by Shelford (1901) to represent only the juvenile plumage of *Philentoma velatum*, the Maroon-breasted Flycatcher (p. 161, fn. 70).

## Summary

This short contribution by Bartlett, then associated with the Sarawak Museum, described what he considered to be a distinct new species of *Philentoma* from Bornean material. The genus *Philentoma* comprises the maroon- and chestnut-winged flycatchers of the Oriental region, and at the time of publication the Bornean representatives were still being sorted out from the closely allied forms of Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula. Bartlett's type specimen(s) were deposited in the Sarawak Museum, where they remained available for subsequent examination (p. 161, fn. 70).

The taxonomic history of this name is instructive of the broader problem Moulton (1914) identifies in his introduction: the "glut of descriptions, based on single specimens, on one sex or on restricted local varieties" that characterized late-Victorian ornithology (p. 126). Shelford's 1901 revision demonstrated that Bartlett's *P. saravacense* was not a valid species but merely the immature plumage of *P. velatum*, a form that differs conspicuously in the colouration of the underparts and wing coverts from the adult (p. 161, fn. 70). Bartlett also described a second *Philentoma*, *P. maxwelli*, which Shelford likewise reduced, identifying it as an aberrant individual of *P. pyrrhopterum* (p. 161, fn. 70).

### Key Findings

- Bartlett described a new *Philentoma* from Borneo in a two-page note (JSBRAS No. 28, 1895, pp. 96–97).
- The type specimen(s) are held in the Sarawak Museum (p. 161, fn. 70).
- Shelford (1901) demonstrated that *P. saravacense* Bartlett is the juvenile form of *P. velatum* Temminck (the Maroon-breasted Flycatcher), not a distinct species (p. 161, fn. 70).
- A second Bartlett species, *P. maxwelli*, was likewise reduced by Shelford to an aberration of *P. pyrrhopterum* Temminck (the Chestnut-winged Flycatcher) (p. 161, fn. 70).
- The British Museum *Hand-List* (1901) had still recorded both of Bartlett's names as valid Bornean species, illustrating the lag between description and revision (p. 161, fn. 70).

### Conclusion

Bartlett's 1895 description is a clear example of the "second stage" of avifaunal list-building that Moulton describes — a period of over-splitting based on limited material, where plumage variation in juveniles or aberrant individuals was interpreted as specific distinction. The subsequent synonymization by Shelford confirms that no new species was involved, and the name is now of purely historical interest.

## Context

- Bartlett was a naturalist working in Sarawak in the 1890s; his types remained in the Sarawak Museum collection, which Moulton (then Curator) had direct access to when writing his 1914 hand-list (p. 161, fn. 70).
- The paper is cited in Moulton's bibliography as reference no. 1 and is the earliest entry in the list, underscoring its role as one of the foundational (if ultimately superseded) contributions to Bornean ornithological nomenclature (p. 177).
