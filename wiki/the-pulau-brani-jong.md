# The Pulau Brani Jong

Eric R. Alfred's 1986 article in the *Journal of the Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* documents three model outrigger canoes (*jong*) from Pulau Brani, Singapore, and argues that their hull form derives from the full-size *Sekochi* of the east coast of Peninsular Malaysia, while their two-masted gunter rig was likely adapted from Indonesian trading vessels that once called at the island.

## Summary

The article addresses a gap in the ethnographic record. Gibson-Hill (1951) had briefly mentioned the existence of model *jong* on Pulau Brani but provided no physical description. Alfred, working from three examples acquired through a former island resident, supplies the first detailed morphological account and systematically compares the Pulau Brani *jong* against two other known forms: the well-documented racing *jong* of Singapore and the Sarawak toy *jong* described by Haddon (1920). The comparison reveals that the Pulau Brani *jong* is distinct from both in hull shape, rig, and outrigger attachment method (p. 136).

The core argument concerns provenance. Alfred tests Gibson-Hill's suggestion that the *jong*'s hull lines derive from the *Sampan Rhio* (or *Sampan Penambang*) and finds the fit imperfect: the *Sampan Rhio* has an angled forefoot and stem/stern heads that finish flush with the gunwales, whereas the *jong* has a curved forefoot and heads that rise well above the gunwales (p. 136). He then demonstrates a near-identical hull correspondence with the *Sekochi*, a boat of the east coast of Peninsular Malaysia whose former range extended to Singapore (p. 137). The two-masted gunter rig, however, is alien to the *Sekochi* and instead matches the rig of Indonesian *Nade* and *Lambo* trading vessels, suggesting that Pulau Brani's role as a stopping point for Indonesian barter traders provided the source for the rig (p. 137).

The article also contributes to the broader question of whether model outrigger canoes in the region reflect the former use of outriggers on full-size vessels, a hypothesis advanced by Hornell (1946). Alfred notes that full-size single-outrigger canoes are unknown in Malaysia and Singapore, and the presence of outrigger attachments on the *jong* lends further support to this theory (p. 138).

### Key Findings

- Three Pulau Brani *jong* examples were acquired in January 1980 by the Maritime Museum, Singapore (Cat. No. SM 074 among them), with overall lengths of 82.5–92.0 cm and hull widths of 13.0–15.5 cm (p. 133).
- The hulls were carved from *Pulai* wood (*Alstonia scholaris*), sourced primarily from beach debris; sails were white cotton cloth and rigging was cotton string (pp. 133, 134).
- The racing *jong* (five examples in the Maritime Museum collection) ranges from 123.0–254.0 cm overall length, substantially larger than the Pulau Brani *jong*, and has a hull length-to-width ratio of 8.2–9.4 compared to 5.2–5.6 for the Pulau Brani form (p. 136).
- The *Sekochi*, the closest full-size hull match, has a maximum recorded length of 9–10 metres, though Alfred observed larger examples of 14–16 metres at Beserah, Pahang in 1975 (p. 137).
- Mr. Ahamad bin Osman, a 55-year-old former resident, dated the tradition to at least the late 1930s; by Alfred's first visit in 1974 the practice had ceased and only one kampong remained on the island (p. 137).
- The outrigger boom is slotted through metal staples on diagonal strips of wood attached to the thwart, a method distinct from the racing *jong*'s deck-mounted strips (p. 136).

### Conclusion

Alfred's definitive takeaway is that the Pulau Brani *jong* represents a hybrid: its hull form is a faithful copy of the larger Malaysian *Sekochi*, while its two-masted gunter rig was borrowed from Indonesian trading vessels that frequented the island. The *jong* was never raced or sailed but produced purely as ornamental souvenirs for sale alongside seashells, coral, and driftwood, and the tradition had already died out by the mid-1970s (p. 137).

## Context

- Primary sources include the Maritime Museum, Singapore collection (3 Pulau Brani *jong*, 5 racing *jong*, 2 full-size *Sekochi*, 1 full-size *Sampan Rhio*), a 1928 photograph of a *Sampan Penambang* held at the Science Museum, London, and the Skeat Collection at Cambridge (pp. 136–137).
- The article fills a specific gap left by Gibson-Hill's passing mention (1951) and provides the first physical description and comparative analysis of the Pulau Brani *jong*, contributing to the broader historiography of model canoe racing and the distribution of outrigger technology in the Strait of Malaya.
