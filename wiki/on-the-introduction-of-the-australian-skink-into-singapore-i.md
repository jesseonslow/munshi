# On the introduction of the Australian skink into Singapore Island. Records of the Raffles Museum, No. 12

This short note by F. N. Chasen, published in the *Journal of the Malayan Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (Vol. 3, No. 1, 1925), documents the discovery of the Australian scink *Egernia depressa* in Singapore and places it within a broader discussion of artificially introduced reptile and amphibian species on the island.

Chasen begins by reviewing the introduction of the bull-frog *Kaloula pulchra* and the tokay gecko *Gekko verticillatus* into Singapore, noting their confinement to urban areas as evidence of artificial introduction (p. 100). He then turns to the Australian scink, reporting that a specimen of *Egernia depressa* (Gunth.) was captured near the Tanjong Pagar docks, and that four other *Egernia* specimens in the Raffles Museum were presumably also taken on the island (p. 100–101). The species is described as roughly the size of the common garden scink *Mabuia multifasciata* but distinguished by its stumpy tail and rows of backwardly directed spines. Chasen attributes the introduction to timber-carrying boats from south-western Australian ports, and notes the species' confinement to the dock area as evidence it had not yet spread beyond its point of arrival (p. 101).
