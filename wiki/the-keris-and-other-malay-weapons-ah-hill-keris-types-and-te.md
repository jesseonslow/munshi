# The keris and other Malay weapons, A.H. Hill; keris types and terms, Geoffrey Hodgson

"A Rice-Ceremony" is a brief field note by R. O. Winstedt, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*, No. 77 (1917), documenting a mock combat ritual he witnessed at Johol.

## Summary

Winstedt records his observation of a *bapua* (or *singketa*) ceremony, a mock combat performed to expel evil influences from rice fields before planting. Two parties hurled *puar* rods—approximately 3½ feet long, thin, green, and straight, with a flat pared root end—across a gully at one another until one combatant was struck in the face and bled, ending the affair after about half an hour (p. 249). A *pawang* opened the proceedings with an invocation.

Winstedt situates the term *bapua* as the Minangkabau pronunciation of *berpuar*, referencing earlier accounts by C. O. Blagden (1896) and W. W. Skeat's *Malay Magic*, and notes that in Selangor the same mock combat is called *singketa*. He adds that *singketa* may refer to a more serious variant using heavier weapons, possibly *batang pisang* (p. 249).
