# Ceremonies at seedtime

A.W. O'Sullivan's "Ceremonies at Seedtime" (JSBRAS Vol. 18, 1886, pp. 362–365) documents Malay propitiatory invocations and offerings performed at the sowing of *padi*, drawing on earlier material from Captain Low's 1836 work and on informants in Penang.

The note presents four invocations in Malay with English renderings: the first addresses Dangomala and Dangomali, spirits of the sun and moon, requesting protection and growth for the crop; the second is a hyperbolical description of the nine months of grain maturation, culminating in a comparison of the harvest with the birth of Mohamed; the third invokes the earth spirit Noh and the air sprite Dewa Imbang; and the fourth addresses Setia Guni, an earth spirit, using the metaphor of a parent entrusting a child to a foster mother for six months (pp. 362–364). O'Sullivan also describes the material offerings—egg, betel leaves, *bras kunnet*, *bras bertik*, and a *ketupat*—and the ritual sequence of sprinkling rich earth and scattering rice into the air, concluding that *pawangs* (wise men skilled in these incantations) are in great demand at sowing time (p. 365).
