# The pied cuckoo-shrike. Records of the Raffles Museum, No. 16

C.B. Kloss (1926) presents a taxonomic revision of the subspecies of the Pied Cuckoo-Shrike *Lalage nigra*, correcting errors in Stuart Baker's 1923 treatment and establishing a new name for the Nicobar form (p. 158).

Kloss argues that Baker's 1923 revision is unacceptable on several grounds: Hellmayr had already shown that *Turdus sueurii* Vieillot has priority over *Ceblepyris timorensis* S. Mull. for the Lesser Sunda and Celebesian form, and Stresemann had demonstrated that *L. n. schisticeps* Neumann is an artifact (a composite of a *Lalage* body and a *Pericrocotus* head) (p. 158). Kloss further shows that *schisticeps* is not confined to the Philippines but also occurs in Borneo, based on Raffles Museum collections from Labuan, Jesselton, and Kuching (pp. 159–160). He describes the juvenile plumage of three races—*nigra*, *schisticeps*, and *sueurii*—noting differences in the extent of barring and the development of adult plumage (pp. 160–161). The note closes with a general caution that the vague locality phrase "India Orientali" used by eighteenth-century authors was a comprehensive term covering the area east of the Arabian Sea, and that type localities fixed on such material require careful interpretation (p. 161).
