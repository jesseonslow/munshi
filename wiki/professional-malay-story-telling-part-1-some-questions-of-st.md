# Professional Malay story-telling. Part 1: some questions of style and presentation

A. Sweeney's "Professional Malay story-telling. Part 1: some questions of style and presentation" (1973) is a study of the oral narrative traditions of the Malay world, examining the stylistic conventions, performance contexts, and presentational techniques of professional storytellers. The article, published in the Journal of the Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, addresses questions of how these narratives were structured, delivered, and received within their cultural settings.

## Summary

Sweeney's article investigates the professional Malay storytelling tradition as a distinct literary and performative genre, focusing on the formal and stylistic features that distinguish it from other modes of oral or written narrative. The study draws on primary source materials to examine how professional storytellers (pencerita) constructed their performances, the conventions governing their delivery, and the relationship between the teller and the audience.

The article addresses several interrelated questions of style and presentation: the linguistic register employed, the use of formulaic expressions and stock phrases, the structural patterns governing narrative progression, and the physical and social context of performance. Sweeney situates these features within the broader framework of Malay literary culture, considering how professional storytelling related to other narrative forms such as the hikayat, the pantun, and the syair.

The study is grounded in close analysis of narrative texts and performance accounts, allowing Sweeney to identify recurring patterns in how professional storytellers managed their material, adapted to different audiences, and maintained the conventions of the genre. The article contributes to understanding the Malay oral tradition not merely as a repository of stories but as a sophisticated performative art with its own internal logic and aesthetic principles.

### Key Findings

- The article identifies specific stylistic markers that distinguish professional Malay storytelling from amateur or domestic narrative, including particular formulaic openings, transitions, and closings that signal the professional status of the teller.
- Sweeney documents the physical presentation conventions of professional storytellers, including posture, gesture, and vocal modulation, which formed an integral part of the narrative experience.
- The study examines the social and economic context of professional storytelling, including the relationship between tellers and patrons, and the occasions on which professional narratives were commissioned or performed.
- Sweeney identifies structural patterns in professional Malay narratives, including the use of frame narratives, embedded stories, and cyclical repetitions that served both mnemonic and aesthetic functions.
- The article considers the linguistic features of the storytelling register, including the use of archaic vocabulary, specific grammatical constructions, and the interweaving of prose and verse passages.

### Conclusion

Sweeney's study establishes that professional Malay storytelling was a highly codified performative tradition with distinct stylistic and presentational conventions that set it apart from other forms of Malay narrative. The article demonstrates that the professional storyteller operated within a well-defined set of expectations regarding language, structure, and delivery, and that these conventions served both artistic and social functions within Malay cultural life.

## Context

- The article draws on Malay narrative texts and performance accounts to reconstruct the conventions of professional storytelling, contributing to the historiography of Malay oral literature.
- Published in JMBRAS Vol. 46 (1973), the article represents a scholarly engagement with Malay literary traditions during a period of renewed academic interest in Southeast Asian oral cultures.
