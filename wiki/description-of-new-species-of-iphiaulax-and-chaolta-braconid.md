# Description of new species of Iphiaulax and Chaolta (Braconidae) from Sarawak, Borneo

P. Cameron published this taxonomic monograph in 1905 in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*, documenting a substantial array of new parasitic Hymenoptera species collected from Sarawak, Borneo. The paper describes approximately 28 new species of *Iphiaulax* and three new species of *Chaolta* (family Braconidae), organized into morphological groups, and represents one of the more extensive single-author contributions to Bornean Hymenoptera taxonomy in the early colonial period.

## Summary

Cameron's paper is a systematic taxonomic treatment of Braconid wasps collected primarily by Mr. Shelford from localities in Sarawak, including Kuching, Matang, Mount Siban, Santubong, and Pampat. The *Iphiaulax* species are arranged into nine informal groups (labelled a through i) defined by combinations of wing colouration, abdominal sculpture, and body colour patterns—ranging from luteous forms with large yellowish-hyaline wings to black species with red heads and thoraces and uniformly dark fuscous wings. Each species description follows a consistent structure: a brief diagnostic summary, measurements (body length and ovipositor/terebra length), locality and season, then a detailed morphological account covering antennal structure, facial sculpture, thoracic features, abdominal segmentation and striation patterns, and wing venation.

The paper also includes supplementary records of previously described species, notably the male of *I. leptopterus* from Santubong at 2,600 feet and additional localities for *I. reticulatus* from Mount Siban and Matang at 3,200 feet, with notes on intraspecific variation in scape colour, abdominal punctuation, and ovipositor sheath colouration. Cameron explicitly notes that *Iphiaulax* is "one of the largest of the Parasitic Hymenoptera" in the tropics and that Shelford held many additional undescribed species, signalling that the fauna was far from exhaustively known. The *Chaolta* section, though shorter, introduces three species sharing a distinctive trituberculate basal abdominal segment, differentiated by facial markings, scutellar shape, and wing pattern.

### Key Findings

- Approximately 28 new *Iphiaulax* species and 3 new *Chaolta* species are described, all from Sarawak localities (Kuching, Matang, Mount Siban, Santubong, Pampat) (pp. 23–51).
- Body lengths range from 7 mm (*I. cilles*) to 20 mm (*I. megapterus*); ovipositor (terebra) lengths range from 2 mm (*I. pheres*) to 40 mm (*I. mobilis*) (pp. 23, 36, 38).
- The collector Mr. Shelford is credited with the material; collection seasons span March through December, with elevations recorded up to 3,200 feet at Matang (pp. 23, 39).
- *Iphiaulax* is organized into nine morphological groups (a–i) based on wing colour, abdominal sculpture, and body colouration, with the *leptopterus* group (a) and the *shelfordi* group (h) being the most speciose (pp. 23–48).
- *I. reticulatus* is recorded from new localities (Mount Siban, Matang 3,200 ft) with documented variation in scape colour (black or red), abdominal punctuation intensity, and ovipositor sheath apex colour (black or white) (p. 39).
- The *Chaolta* species share a trituberculate base to the second abdominal segment; *C. trituberculata* (11–12 mm, terebra 9 mm) is distinguished from *C. lutea* by its black frontal mark, shorter ovipositor, and black antennal scape (pp. 49–50).

### Conclusion

Cameron's paper establishes the extraordinary species richness of *Iphiaulax* in Sarawak and provides the foundational taxonomy for the genus in Borneo. By organizing the species into coherent morphological groups and documenting variation within previously described taxa, the work creates a reference framework that subsequent Bornean Hymenoptera studies would build upon, while simultaneously underscoring how much of the tropical parasitic wasp fauna remained undescribed.

## Context

- P. Cameron (1857–1945) was a British entomologist stationed in Borneo who produced an enormous body of Hymenoptera taxonomy for the Straits Settlements and Sarawak; this paper is part of a sustained programme of faunal documentation conducted under colonial administrative auspices.
- The primary material derives from the collections of Mr. Shelford, a contemporary collector in Sarawak; the paper draws on no archival or historical sources beyond the specimens themselves.
