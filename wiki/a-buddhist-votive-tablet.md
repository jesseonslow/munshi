# A Buddhist votive tablet

[C. O. Blagden](./charles-otto-blagden.md)'s short note describes a fragmentary clay tablet bearing a 10th-century Nagari inscription, discovered by H. [Vaughan Stevens](./vaughan-stevens.md) in a cave in [Kedah](./kedah.md) and now held in the [Singapore](./singapore.md) Museum (p. 205).

Blagden reports that Professor Kern of Leyden, examining a photograph of the tablet, identified the script as Nagari of approximately the 10th century and recognised parts of the well-known Buddhist formula *ye dharmā hetu prabhavā* at the top, though he was unable to decipher the whole (p. 205). Blagden contrasts this tablet's Northern Indian script with other [Kedah](./kedah.md) and [Province Wellesley](./province-wellesley.md) [inscriptions](./inscriptions.md) in a South Indian [alphabet](./alphabet.md), which Kern had dated to c. 400 A.D., and concludes that both Northern and Southern [India](./india.md) contributed to the civilisation of the Malayan regions (p. 205). He further argues that the Peninsular [inscriptions](./inscriptions.md), though few, refute [Hugh Clifford](./sir-hugh-charles-clifford.md)'s claim that Hindu influence did not extend to the Peninsula, and that Indian influence was the most potent force in the civilisation of the Javanese and Malays (p. 206).
