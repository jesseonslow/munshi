# The Sumatran rhinoceros

This page of Short Notes from JSBRAS Vol. 35 (1901) contains three brief items, the last of which—on the Sumatran rhinoceros—is attributed to H.N. Ridley in the publication metadata.

## Summary

The page opens with a note on the habits of the racket-tailed Drongo (*Dissemurus platyurus*), explaining that the bird follows troops of long-tailed macaques (*Macacus cynomolgus*) to catch insects disturbed by the monkeys' movement through foliage, a relationship the Malays describe as "Hamba Kerah" (slave of the monkey) (p. 105). A second note records the capture of a short-eared owl (*Asio accipitrinus*) in Alexandra Road, Singapore, in December 1900, identified by A. L. Butler, and notes that this species, though widely distributed from Europe to India and Ceylon, had not previously been recorded from the Malay Peninsula (p. 105). The third note, on the Sumatran rhinoceros, observes that while both *R. javanicus* and *R. sumatrensis* occur in the Malay Peninsula, records of where the different species were obtained are lacking and preserved specimens are rare; it then begins to report that two examples of *R. sumatrensis* have recently been obtained, but the text is cut off at this point (p. 105).
