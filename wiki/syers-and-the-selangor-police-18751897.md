# Syers and the Selangor Police, 1875–1897

Mubin Sheppard's 1978 obituary commemorates Tan Sri Dato Haji Nik Ahmed Kamil bin Nik Mahmood (1909–1977), the eldest son of Kelantan's first modern Chief Minister, who served as President of the Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society from 1964 until his death. The piece traces Nik Kamil's extraordinary career from colonial-era state secretary through wartime administration, diplomatic service in London and Washington, and a post-independence business career, framing his life as an exemplar of the Kiplingian virtues quoted in the opening epigraph.

## Summary

Sheppard's memoir is structured as a chronological narrative of Nik Kamil's public life, beginning with his birth in Kota Bharu in 1909 and his education at Malay College Kuala Kangsar and Lincoln's Inn, where he was called to the Bar in 1930 at age twenty. The account then follows his rapid rise through the Kelantan Civil Service — State Secretary by 1934, deputy Menteri Besar by 1938, and Menteri Besar himself from late 1947 — through the Malayan Union controversy, the Emergency, and his appointment as Member for Lands, Mines and Communications in the federal government in 1953.

The second phase of the narrative covers Nik Kamil's diplomatic career: Malayan Trade Commissioner in Australia, High Commissioner in London (1957), and Ambassador to the United States and Permanent Representative to the United Nations (1959). After a brief electoral defeat in 1955 and a stint in private legal practice, he returned to politics as a Member of Parliament for Ulu Kelantan (1969–1974) and was elected Speaker of the Dewan Rakyat in 1974, a post he held until his fatal heart attack in the chamber.

The final paragraphs shift from public achievement to personal character, noting his patronage of traditional Malay performing arts (Ma'yong, Ronggeng, Mak Inang, Zapin), his directorships across fifty-six companies at the time of his death, and his sustained involvement with charitable and learned institutions including the World Wildlife Fund, the Outward Bound Trust, and the MBRAS itself.

### Key Findings

- Nik Kamil was called to the Bar in 1930 at age twenty, the first Kelantan Malay to enter Lincoln's Inn (p. 1).
- He was gazetted Menteri Besar of Kelantan at the end of December 1947, succeeding his father as head of state government (p. 1).
- He organised the first anti-communist 'Kampong Guards' in the peninsula following the Emergency proclamation of June 1948 (p. 1).
- He served as Malayan High Commissioner in London from October 1957 and as Ambassador to the U.S.A. and U.N. representative from 1959 (p. 2).
- At the time of his death he sat on the boards of fifty-six companies, including Rothmans of Pall Mall, Dunlops, Shell, Metal Box, and Public Bank (p. 3).
- He was elected Speaker of the Dewan Rakyat in 1974 and died of a heart attack in Parliament (p. 3).

### Conclusion

Sheppard's definitive takeaway is that Nik Kamil's public résumé — spanning colonial administration, diplomacy, politics, and commerce — captures only the outer dimensions of a man whose personal qualities of modesty, wit, and unassuming dignity made him an exceptional figure in Malayan and Malaysian public life. The obituary positions him as a bridge between the traditional Malay aristocracy of Kelantan and the modern federal state, and as a patron whose support was indispensable to institutions like the MBRAS.

## Context

- The obituary was published in the same volume (Vol. 51) of the JMBRAS that Nik Kamil had presided over, and it appears in Part 1 (No. 233) rather than the Part 2 (No. 234) where the metadata title "Syers and the Selangor Police" would be expected; the document provided is the obituary, not the Gullick article.
- The piece draws on Sheppard's personal acquaintance with Nik Kamil and on standard biographical sources; it contributes to the institutional memory of the MBRAS and to the broader historiography of Kelantan's political elite in the colonial and early independence periods.
