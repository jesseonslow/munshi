# Some notes on keris measurements

A brief note by the late G. M. Laidlaw (d. 1905), published posthumously in 1947, describing two traditional Malay systems for measuring keris blades and recording associated blacksmith folklore from Kelantan.

## Summary

The note first gives a method in which the blade is measured with a leaf-strip, halved, and then counted off in blade-breadths at the midpoint; at each breadth the formula "GUNONG : RUNTOH : MADU : SEGARA" is recited, with *gunong* and *madu* being the lucky terms (p. 45). A blade yielding *gunong* thrice (at quarter, half, and three-quarter points) is deemed exceptionally lucky for a trader but valueless to a warrior (*hulubalang*). A second system divides the half-strip into pieces arranged in three forms—*Gagal Lapar*, *Penchebak*, and *Daun Sa-Puchok*—the first and third being favourable to a fighting man, the second contemptuous (p. 46).

The note closes with miscellaneous observations: the vital principle of Malay weapons is termed *maya besi* rather than *semangat*; a Kelantan blacksmith's taboo against stepping over his tools; the auspicious moment for beginning keris manufacture (strong breath through the right nostril); and wartime taboos for a husband's wife, including sleeping only from midnight to two a.m. and restrictions on hair-cutting, oil, and rice-pot stirring (p. 46).
