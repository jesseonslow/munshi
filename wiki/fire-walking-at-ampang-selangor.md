# Fire-walking at Ampang, Selangor

David Freeman's short note describes his observation of an annual Chinese fire-walking ceremony held at a small temple near Ampang, Selangor, on the ninth day of the ninth lunar month (18 October 1923).

## Summary

Freeman, who had lived in Kuala Lumpur for eighteen years without previously witnessing the event, provides a detailed account of the preparations and proceedings. The ceremony involved Hokkien coolies from Fukien Province who had undergone weeks of seclusion and vegetarian diet. The ritual included the construction of a charcoal pyre approximately twelve feet long, the invocation of the god Kow Wong Yeh (Respected Ninth King) carried in an apparently empty sedan chair, the self-mutilation of a fanatic with an axe and dagger, and the tracing of a double Greek cross in the earth by a hierophant. Approximately one hundred barefoot candidates, dressed in white, then ran across the glowing charcoal from north-west to south-east, most taking four steps, with none apparently burnt or injured (pp. 74–76).
