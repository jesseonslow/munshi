# Dialects of the Malay Peninsula

C. O. Blagden's brief note in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (Vol. 37, 1902) is an appeal to members for assistance in collecting and comparing the dialects of the aboriginal tribes of the Malay Peninsula, with a broader call for the systematic recording of local Malay dialects.

Blagden announces that he is engaged in collating dialects of the "Wild Tribes" of the Peninsula and requests contributions of vocabularies, grammatical notes, sentence specimens with verbatim translations, and lists of personal names, each accompanied by the tribe's name, location, and physical characteristics (p. 141). He is particularly in want of material on the aboriginal dialects of Negri Sembilan and Pahang. He further urges members to record local peculiarities of Malay dialects — including intonation, pronunciation of syllables (especially finals), and grammatical or syntactical differences from standard Malay (pp. 141–142) — noting that the Dutch have already compiled far more adequate records of the languages of their portion of the Archipelago. He cites the Menangkabau dialect, spoken in Naning and Negri Sembilan, as an example of a dialect for which no adequate English record exists despite the existence of a Dutch dictionary and literary specimens (p. 142). Finally, he identifies place-names, particularly those with no Malay meaning, as another subject needing investigation, as they may illuminate the pre-Malay condition of the Peninsula (p. 143).
