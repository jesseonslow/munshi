# Taiping (Larut): the early history of a mining settlement

This is a brief typographical correction note by C. O. Blagden, published in the Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, No. 65 (1913), clarifying the transcription of the Kota Kapur Inscription.

## Summary

Blagden addresses a printing limitation in his earlier note on the Kota Kapur Inscription (No. 64 of the same journal), where the typesetter could not distinguish between ordinary *n*, *s*, *h* and their dotted variants. He explains that all words ending in *h* in the inscription were originally written with the Indian *visarga* symbol (transcribed as dotted *h*), which is of no particular etymological significance for Indonesian languages. The dotted *s* (representing *sh*) occurs in *dosa*, properly *dosha*. Most importantly, he clarifies that dotted *n* represents the velar nasal (Romanized *ng*) and re-lists the relevant words—*urang*, *di dalangna*, *pulang*, *mulang*, *mang-*, *mang-i*, *mangujari*, *ding*, *dngan*, *jangan*, *yang*, *iyang*, *grang*—in their corrected Romanized spelling, noting that this makes their close connexion with corresponding Malay equivalents even more apparent (p. 2).
