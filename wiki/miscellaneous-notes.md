# Miscellaneous notes

W.G. Maxwell's "Miscellaneous Notes" (JSBRAS Vol. 57, 1911, pp. 195–196) records a collection of Kelantanese Malay folk lore jottings gathered from an informant named Pa' Senik, an old Kelantan Malay resident in Kinta.

## Summary

The notes document a range of hunting-related beliefs and rituals, including mantras to be recited before and after deer hunts, propitiatory offerings at the forest edge, and the *sapu bahdi* and *labor* ceremonies performed after a deer's death to avert evil influence (p. 195). Maxwell also records the genealogy of the first deer hunters, who upon death became *hantu rusa* (deer spirits), and the belief that the wife of the first hunter, Nang Peluntong Chai, drives deer away if preliminary ceremonies are neglected (p. 195).

The remaining fragments cover protective mantras against *bahdi* (evil influence), snake and scorpion bites, and Sakais' poison, as well as cosmological beliefs including the origin of the gun, the nature of *batu lintar* (thunderbolt stones), the identification of tin ore as the earth's milk and gold as its blood, and the Hindu-influenced belief that the earth rests on the horns of a bull whose tossing causes earthquakes (pp. 196–197).
