# Notes on two knives in the Pitt-Rivers Museum

G.C. Woolley's brief note examines two weapons in the Pitt-Rivers Museum, Oxford, questioning the identification of both as *keris* and arguing for their reclassification based on morphological and functional evidence.

The first knife, from the John Harvey collection (Java, 1845–1847), is a one-piece blade-and-handle weapon whose outline resembles the *tumbok lada* but with a double-edged lower half and a hilt figure facing the edge rather than the side, as in the *keris*. Woolley concludes it cannot be called a *keris* but may represent a talisman of equivalent magic value cast in the *tumbok lada* form (p. 39). The second weapon, labelled "very rare type of *keris* with open work blade and unusual grip? Java," is subjected to more detailed analysis: its asymmetrical blade lacks the characteristic *silang* and *dagu* widenings of true *keris*, its grooves do not flare into the thumb-hollow typical of ordinary blades, and its arched holes have no parallel in genuine *keris* (p. 40). Woolley further notes that the symmetrical *ganja* resembles a rapier or halberd guard, and that the hilt is sized for a European hand and balanced for cutting rather than thrusting. He concludes that the weapon should not be called a *keris* and, if Malay at all, represents a freak or experimental adaptation, possibly of a heavy halberd blade (p. 40).
