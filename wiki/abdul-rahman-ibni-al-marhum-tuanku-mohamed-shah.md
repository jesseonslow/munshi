Abdul Rahman ibni al-Marhum Tuanku Muhammad Shah, styled Tunku Muda Serting prior to his accession, was the eldest son of Tuanku Muhammad, *Yang Di-Pertuan Besar* (the hereditary ruler) of Negri Sembilan, and succeeded to the throne on 3 August 1933 following the unanimous election of the four *Undang* (principal chiefs) of the *Tanah Mengandong* (the four senior districts of the state). His accession, conducted in strict accordance with the constitutional arrangements established under the Agreement of 1898, marked the continuation of a dynasty that had governed Negri Sembilan for nearly half a century under his father. The principal archival record of his proclamation is the contemporaneous account compiled by H. P. Bryson and I. W. Blelloch, both serving members of the Malayan Civil Service, published in 1936 [[2, pp. 272–279]](#ref-2).

### The Death of His Father and the Conclave of the Undang

Tuanku Muhammad died in the *Astana Besar* (the royal palace) at Sri Menanti, the traditional seat of the rulers of Negri Sembilan, shortly before 3 p.m. on Tuesday, 1 August 1933, after an illness lasting only a few days [[2, p. 272]](#ref-2). He had reigned for forty-five years, having been made *Yang Di-Pertuan Besar* in 1898 [[2, p. 278]](#ref-2). Immediately after his death, the Tunku Besar Burhanuddin, the late ruler's eldest brother, summoned the four *Penghulu* (district chiefs) of the *Tanah Mengandong*—the Dato's of Ulu Muar, Jempul, Terachi, and Gunong Pasir—and informed them of the death of the *Yang Di-Pertuan Besar* [[2, p. 272]](#ref-2). A salute of twenty-two guns was fired, and outside the *Astana* the royal umbrellas (*payong kuning*), the tufted spears (*tombak benderang*), and pennons of alternately red and yellow were set up [[2, p. 272]](#ref-2).

The news was then formally conveyed to the four *Undang* through the principal *Lembaga* (subordinate officials) of Ulu Muar. The four *Undang*—the Dato' Klana of Sungai Ujong, the Dato' Jelebu, the Dato' Johol, and the Dato' Rembau—met in conclave in Seremban on the morning of 2 August 1933 [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2). Upon conclusion of their deliberations, the Dato' Klana informed the British Resident, through the Secretary to Resident, that the *Undang* awaited his presence [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2). The Resident entered the Council Chamber and, as a preliminary, read to the *Undang* section 6 of the Agreement of 1898, enquiring whether they had reached unanimity [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2). The Dato' Klana then announced that they had, in conference, unanimously agreed on the election of Tunku Abdul Rahman, the eldest son of the deceased Tuanku Muhammad [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2). This decision was, with the consent of the *Undang*, conveyed by the Resident to His Excellency the High Commissioner and to the Chief Secretary to Government [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2).

The constitutional mechanism thus described reflects the dual character of Negri Sembilan's governance: the *Undang* retained the traditional right of election, while the British Resident's role under the 1898 Agreement provided a formal check on the process, ensuring that the election was unanimous and communicated to the colonial authorities [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2).

### The Proclamation and Accession Ceremony

The proclamation of the successor took place on 3 August 1933 in the *balai penghadapan* (audience hall) of the *Astana* [[2, p. 274]](#ref-2). The throne stood on a dais set on a platform occupying one end of the hall, approached by seven steps [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2). The seating arrangement was strictly prescribed: to the left of the throne sat the four principal court officials (*Anak Putra Yang Ampat*)—Tunku Besar, Tunku Muda Serting (Abdul Rahman himself, prior to his accession), Tunku Panglima Besar, and Tunku Lakasmana—together with Tunku Sulaiman, with other near relatives of the deceased ruler behind them [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2). On the right side of the throne sat the Chief Secretary to Government, the British Resident of Negri Sembilan, and the four *Undang* in order of precedence: the Dato' Klana of Sungai Ujong, the Dato' Jelebu, the Dato' Johol, and the Dato' Rembau [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2). Below them were the representatives of the High Commissioner, the Sultans of Perak, Selangor, and Johore, the British Resident of Perak, and the Raja Muda of Selangor [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2).

The Dato' Bentara Dalam (a senior court official) advanced to the front of the platform and called for silence [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2). The Dato' Klana, standing on the right below the throne, addressed the princes of the blood, announcing that the four *Undang* had unanimously chosen Tunku Abdul Rahman, the Tunku Muda Serting, to be *Yang Di-Pertuan Besar* [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2). The text of this announcement is preserved in Malay in Appendix B of the Bryson and Blelloch record [[2, p. 278]](#ref-2).

The Tunku Lakasmana then communicated the decision to Tunku Abdul Rahman, who, after his shoes had been removed by the Lakasmana, ascended the dais and took his seat upon the throne [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2). The four *Undang*, in their order of precedence, made obeisance (*menghadap*) to the new *Yang Di-Pertuan Besar*, bowing from each of the seven steps leading to the throne, and from five only on retreat [[2, p. 275]](#ref-2). Following the *Undang* came the Dato' Shah Bandar of Sungai Ujong, the Dato' Ulu Muar, the Dato' Jempul, the Dato' Terachi, the Dato' Gunong Pasir, the Dato' Muda Linggi, the Dato' Inas, and the Dato' Gemencheh, all of whom made obeisance nine times advancing and seven times on retiring [[2, pp. 275–276]](#ref-2).

There was some doubt, recorded by Bryson and Blelloch, whether the princes (*anak putera*, the princes of the blood) should make obeisance on this occasion, and if so, at what point in the order of precedence they should come; possibly inability to reach a satisfactory decision on the latter point was responsible for the decision to omit them [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2).

On completion of the *menghadap*, the Dato' Klana made the ceremonial presentation of *sireh* (betel quid), contained in a box wrapped in gold cloth, from which the new ruler extracted and ate a shoot [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2). He then proffered a packet, similarly wrapped, containing the *mas manah* (the ceremonial offering) of the four *Undang*, which amounted to $384, being four *bahara* (units of offering) of $24 each from each *Undang* [[2, pp. 275]](#ref-2). The Dato' Bentara Dalam, making full obeisance, then proffered a document on his head, and the new *Yang Di-Pertuan Besar* read a short address [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2). After that, he shook hands with the various officers, chiefs, and representatives, and the more senior of the *anak putera* and *anak putri* (princes and princesses of the blood) [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2).

### The Accession Address

The address delivered by Tunku Abdul Rahman on 3 August 1933 is preserved in full in Malay in Appendix C of the Bryson and Blelloch record [[2, pp. 278–279]](#ref-2). In it, the new ruler expressed the depth of his grief at the death of his father, Tuanku Muhammad Shah, G.C.M.G. and K.C.V.O., stating that his sorrow could not be adequately expressed in words [[2, p. 278]](#ref-2). He acknowledged that his father had reigned for forty-five years with justice and generosity, and that the progress which had taken place in the state was evident to all [[2, p. 278]](#ref-2).

He then addressed the weight of his new responsibility, stating that he was aware of the great and heavy obligation he now bore, and that he did not believe he could fully follow the justice of the late ruler [[2, p. 279]](#ref-2). Nevertheless, he asked the *Undang*, the princes, and the honourable advisers to be assured that he would, with all his ability, administer justice for the security and safety of all subjects and those dwelling throughout the realm of Negri Sembilan [[2, p. 279]](#ref-2).

Two further commitments of particular constitutional significance were made. First, he affirmed that the friendship between Great Britain and Negri Sembilan would remain unchanged [[2, p. 279]](#ref-2). Second, he affirmed that the protection of the state under Great Britain would not be altered in any degree [[2, p. 279]](#ref-2). These declarations, delivered in the context of the 1898 Agreement, served to reaffirm the constitutional relationship between the state and the British colonial administration at the moment of dynastic transition.

### Research and Documentation

The principal archival record of Abdul Rahman's accession is the account compiled by H. P. Bryson and I. W. Blelloch, both members of the Malayan Civil Service, published in the *Journal of the Malayan Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* in 1936 [[2, pp. 272–279]](#ref-2). The document is explicitly an administrative record of proceedings rather than a scholarly analysis; the authors drew on their direct observation of the events and on the Malay-language appendices, which constitute the primary archival evidence for the ceremonial language used [[2, pp. 272–279]](#ref-2). The appendices preserve the Dato' Klana's announcement of the election (Appendix B) and the new ruler's accession address (Appendix C) in their original Malay, providing invaluable primary source material for the study of Negri Sembilan's constitutional and ceremonial traditions [[2, pp. 278–279]](#ref-2).

The record is notable for its attention to the precise sequence of ceremonial acts, the seating arrangements, the order of precedence, and the specific quantities of offerings, all of which reflect the authors' intent to produce an authoritative administrative account [[2, pp. 274–276]](#ref-2). Footnotes within the record provide additional context on the positioning of certain officials, including the Dato' Shah Bandar of Sungai Ujong, whose high position in the order of precedence was given at the express wish of the Dato' Klana, and the Dato' Muda Linggi, who was raised above the Dato' Inas for similar reasons [[2, p. 276]](#ref-2).

The limits of the available record should be noted. The Bryson and Blelloch account documents the events of 1, 2, and 3 August 1933 in considerable detail but provides no biographical information on Abdul Rahman prior to his accession, no account of his subsequent reign, and no assessment of his governance. The second record in the Society's literature for this period, J. J. Sheehan's account of the installation of Tengku Kursiah as *Tengku Ampuan* (Queen) of Negri Sembilan, published in the same volume of the journal, does not provide additional biographical detail on the new ruler [[1, pp. 243–246]](#ref-1). Consequently, the present article is confined to the events and documents attested in the archival record of his proclamation.
