# Asiatic studies, by Dutch societies in 1885

This brief note by [R. Rost](./r-rost.md) [surveys](./surveys.md) publications of Dutch learned societies in 1885 that are of interest to the [Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society](./malaysian-branch-of-the-royal-asiatic-society.md), focusing on the *Bijdragen* of the Royal Institute of The Hague, *De Indische Gids*, and the *Tijdschrift voor Nederlandsch Indie* (p. 439).

The note highlights three articles in the *Bijdragen*: Professor Kern's paper on Philippine alphabets and their affinity to those of [Sumatra](./sumatra.md), [Java](./java.md), [Celebes](./celebes.md), and Kamboja; Dr. Wilken's study of circumcision in the Indian Archipelago, arguing it is unrelated to the Moslem rite; and a 156-page legend of Prince Sutan Manangkera in the Manangkarkau [dialect](./dialects-and-dictionaries.md), contributed by Mr. Van der Toorn of Fort de Kock (p. 439). Rost then catalogues the accumulated Manangkarkau materials—texts, translations, glossaries, riddles, pantuns, and proverbs—published across the Batavian *Tijdschrift*, the *Verhandelingen van het Bataviaasch Genootschap*, and the *Bijdragen*, concluding that sufficient material now exists for a conspectus of the language (pp. 439–440). He also notes Wilken's concluding articles on spirit worship in [Malaysia](./malaysia.md) and Polynesia in *De Indische Gids*, and a series by J. A. B. Wiselins on [prisons](./prisons.md) and forced [labour](./labour.md) in British [India](./india.md) and the Dutch possessions, of which only a few pages concern the [Straits Settlements](./straits-settlements.md) (p. 440).
