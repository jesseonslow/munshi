# A note on the Tagals of Sarawak

A brief ethnographic note by E. V. Andreini describing the customs, beliefs, and material culture of the Tagal people of Sarawak.

## Summary

Andreini documents the Tagals' distinctive amusement called "Ungakang," in which young men jump on spring boards in the long house verandah while women in their best dress lock-step around them on the swaying boards, a practice he identifies as a survival of a head dance (p. 217). He notes their artistic inclinations in door ornamentation, bamboo pipes, and combs, as well as their fine chorus singing, and records their practice of tattooing as a means of providing light when the eyes close in death.

The note also records Tagal cosmogonic narratives — including one in which the Sun laid three eggs producing a Murut, a Tagal, and a third people, and another in which the first man dug holes for rivers and whose youngest child was drowned in the Runi — as well as their funerary practice of placing beads in the mouth of the dead to deceive a snake-guardian at the entrance to the dead man's country (p. 217). Andreini further observes their belief in bird omens limited to the first two days of a journey, the attribution of head-hunting's origin to women, certain women's refusal to eat deer's flesh as reincarnation of the dead, and the robust construction of their houses relative to surrounding Murut dwellings.
