# A history of Selangor

The metadata identifies this document as [R.O. Winstedt](./richard-olaf-winstedt.md)'s "A [history](./history.md) of [Selangor](./selangor.md)" ([JMBRAS](./journal-of-the-malaysian-branch-of-the-royal-asiatic-society.md) Vol. 12, 1934), but the primary source text provided is in fact the **Catalogue of Books, &c., in the Library of the [Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society](./malaysian-branch-of-the-royal-asiatic-society.md) (January 1884)** — a library inventory, not a historical monograph. The summary below reflects the text actually supplied.

## Summary

The document is a bibliographic catalogue of the library holdings of the [Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society](./malaysian-branch-of-the-royal-asiatic-society.md), compiled as of January 1884. It lists approximately 150–200 volumes and serials held by the Society's library in [Singapore](./singapore.md), organised alphabetically by author or corporate body. The collection spans a wide range of subjects relevant to the colonial and scholarly community of the [Straits Settlements](./straits-settlements.md): [ethnography](./ethnography.md), geography, [natural history](./natural-history.md), linguistics, and the history and administration of the [Malay](./malay.md) Archipelago, [Southeast Asia](./southeast-asia.md), and the broader Pacific.

The holdings reveal the intellectual preoccupations of the late-Victorian colonial research establishment. A substantial proportion of the collection consists of German and Dutch ethnographic and geographic publications (Bastian, Meyer, Miklucho-Maclay, Robide van der Aa, the Bataviaasch Genootschap catalogues), reflecting the strong influence of [European](./european.md) scientific societies on colonial knowledge production. Serials from the Paris Société de Géographie, the Bremen Geographische Gesellschaft, and the Bataviaasch Genootschap dominate the periodical holdings, alongside a few local and regional titles such as the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (Nos. 1–11) and *Eastern Asia* (Singapore, 1875).

Several entries are of particular local significance: the *[Hikayat Abdullah](./hikayat-abdullah.md)* (published in the *Annals de l'Extrême Orient*, 1883), the *Maritime Code of the Malays* (Singapore, 1877), *Makota Radja-Radja* (Bokhari of [Johore](./johor.md), translated by Marbe, Paris 1878), and *[Singapore](./singapore.md) Sixty Years Ago* (1883). These represent the Society's engagement with indigenous [Malay](./malay.md) literary and legal [traditions](./traditions.md), even as the bulk of the collection is [European](./european.md)-authored.

### Key Findings

- The catalogue covers holdings as of January 1884 and includes serials with specific missing-number annotations (e.g., *Bulletin de la Société de Géographie de Paris*: missing Jan. and Nov. 1879; Jan. and Feb. 1880) (pp. 3–4).
- The *[Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society](./journal-of-the-malaysian-branch-of-the-royal-asiatic-society.md)* is listed at Nos. 1–11, indicating the Society had published eleven issues by this date (p. 5).
- The *[Hikayat Abdullah](./hikayat-abdullah.md)* is recorded in its 1883 French publication in the *Annals de l'Extrême Orient* (Nos. 55, 58–65; Nos. 56–57 missing) (p. 2).
- The collection includes the *Maritime Code of the Malays* (Singapore, 1877, 2 copies) and *Makota Radja-Radja* (Paris, 1878), evidencing early institutional interest in Malay legal and literary texts (pp. 5–6).
- German-language ethnographic and zoological works (Meyer, Miklucho-Maclay, Bastian) and Dutch colonial publications (Bataviaasch Genootschap, Robide van der Aa) constitute a large share of the holdings, underscoring the transnational character of colonial scientific networks (pp. 2–8).
- The catalogue includes works on [New Guinea](./new-guinea.md), the [Philippines](./philippines.md), [Borneo](./borneo.md), and the Malay Peninsula, reflecting the Society's geographic scope across the broader Archipelago rather than a narrow [Straits Settlements](./straits-settlements.md) focus (pp. 4–12).

### Conclusion

The 1884 catalogue documents the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society as a modest but cosmopolitan research library, its holdings shaped by the European (particularly German and Dutch) scientific establishment of the 1870s–1880s. It provides a snapshot of what colonial-era scholars in Singapore considered worth collecting: a mix of European ethnographic theory, geographic serials, and a small but significant core of Malay-language primary texts.

## Context

- This is a front-matter/inventory document (library catalogue) from the early history of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, predating the Malaysian Branch by several decades.
- The catalogue is a useful prosopographical and intellectual-history source for understanding the research infrastructure and epistemic priorities of the colonial-era RAS in Singapore.
