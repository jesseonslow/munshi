# The survey question

W.E. Maxwell, an officer of the Straits Settlements Survey Department, published this piece in 1886 as a translation of a paper by M. Camouilly from the *Bulletin de la Société des Études Indo-Chinoises de Saigon*, presenting a detailed French colonial argument against a full cadastral survey in Cochin-China and in favour of the Torrens system of voluntary title registration. Maxwell, who personally advocated for a cadastral survey, republished the paper to expose its internal contradictions to his Society's members (p. 271).

## Summary

The document comprises Maxwell's brief preface (pp. 271–272) and Camouilly's full argument (pp. 273–291). Maxwell frames the issue by noting that the "Survey Question" had been a live controversy in the Straits Settlements since 1883, culminating in a government report by Lieut.-Colonel Barron of the Survey of India, and that parallel debates were now occurring in Cochin-China (p. 271). He translates Camouilly's paper not in agreement but to test its logic: if the destruction of the Annamite communal revenue system is a valid reason to reject a cadastral survey, the same argument applies with equal force to the Torrens system of individual title registration that Camouilly simultaneously advocates (p. 271).

Camouilly's paper is a sustained fiscal and administrative critique. He reviews the history of the French *cadastre*—a work that took over forty years and cost 160 million francs, yet still produced complaints of inequitable assessment—and argues that no analogous undertaking is feasible in Cochin-China (pp. 276–277). He then presents two official cost estimates: M. Bataille's (1879) figure of 6,048,000 francs for 650,000 hectares over 16 years, and M. Boilloux's more comprehensive estimate of 42,517,444 francs (survey plus maintenance capital) for 3,600,000 hectares, which Camouilly himself revises downward to approximately 16,000,000 francs for a more realistic 1,450,000 hectares (pp. 279–284). His definitive verdict is that the survey "will never be finished" and that whatever portion is completed "will never be kept up" (p. 284).

The positive proposal is the Torrens system, which Camouilly sees as a natural extension of existing Annamite practice of issuing title-deeds. He argues that native landholders are victims of extreme usury, that government-certified titles would enable them to borrow at reasonable rates from respectable institutions, and that only those holdings whose owners voluntarily apply need be surveyed and demarcated (pp. 289–291). Maxwell's preface closes by expressing his own scepticism about the difficulties Camouilly invokes, noting that British India's experience contradicts the fear that cadastral survey destroys communal revenue collection (p. 272).

### Key Findings

- The Annamite communal system collects land revenue from 2,450 municipalities without documents, prosecutions, or expense; the difference between actual cultivated area and revenue-paid area in surveyed arrondissements is approximately one-fortieth (p. 286).
- M. Boilloux's 1879 estimate for a full cadastral survey of Cochin-China totals 42,517,444 francs (23,304,784 francs for the survey itself plus 19,212,660 francs capitalised maintenance), equivalent to 10 francs 75 centimes per hectare (pp. 279–280).
- Camouilly's own revised estimate, based on 950,000 hectares currently cultivated plus 500,000 hectares projected over 16 years, yields a total cost of approximately 16,000,000 francs at 11 francs per hectare (pp. 283–284).
- The topographical survey at 1/20,000 scale, undertaken over fourteen years, had measured approximately 730,000 hectares of cultivable land by December 1884, with a further 70,000 hectares in arrondissements not yet surveyed (p. 282).
- Rice exports rose from an average of 5,165,000 piculs (1881–82) to 8,538,000 piculs (1883–84), an increase Camouilly attributes to an expansion of cultivated area by approximately 152,000 hectares rather than improved yields (pp. 282–283).
- In arrondissements subject to individual assessment (the *vingtième* system), the Government expends approximately forty per cent of the sum realised in the cost of collection (p. 287).

### Conclusion

Camouilly's definitive historical takeaway is that the French colonial administration should preserve the Annamite commune as the unit of revenue collection, reject the cadastral survey as both unaffordable and self-defeating, and instead introduce the Torrens system on a voluntary, demand-driven basis to address the acute credit needs of native landholders without disrupting the communal fiscal structure that makes governance of Cochin-China comparatively inexpensive and stable (pp. 285–291). Maxwell, while disagreeing with this conclusion, concedes the paper's value in forcing a rigorous examination of the assumptions underlying both positions (p. 272).

## Context

- The paper was originally published in the *Bulletin de la Société des Études Indo-Chinoises de Saigon* for the first half-year of 1886, a society active in promoting French colonial policy in the new protectorates (p. 273).
- Maxwell's decision to republish in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* reflects the parallel "Survey Question" then being debated in the Straits Settlements, where a government report by Lieut.-Colonel Barron of the Survey of India had just been issued (p. 271).
