# Special Thai-Malaysian relations

R. O. Winstedt's "The Teaching of Malay in Europe" (1917) is a short article advocating for the value of preliminary Malay-language instruction in European institutions, written in the context of the newly established School of Oriental Studies in London.

## Summary

Winstedt opens by quoting Sir Charles Lyall's 1907 memorandum to the committee on oriental studies in London, which argued that European students benefit from home-based training in Oriental languages because of the guidance of European teachers, access to comparative libraries, and the pedagogical shortcomings of local *munshis* (p. 2). He notes that the committee's recommendations led to the opening of the School of Oriental Studies at Finsbury Circus in February 1917, with C. O. Blagden appointed as its first Lecturer in Malay (p. 3).

The article then surveys existing provision for Malay studies at the École Spéciale des Langues Orientales Vivantes in Paris and at Leiden University, where courses serve candidates for the Dutch colonial civil service, magistrates, and doctoral students in comparative linguistics (pp. 3–4). Winstedt closes by urging that commercial firms, estates, and civil-service cadets in the Malay Peninsula should make use of the London School's facilities, and asserts that a standard of scholarship in Malay studies exists and should be recognised, dispelling "the hallowed notion that the highest authority on Malay matters is a *kampong* elder" (p. 4).
