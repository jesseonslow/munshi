# The mountain system of the Malayan Peninsula (1884

This is a reprint of a letter by J. E. Tenison-Woods, originally published in *Nature* on 17 July 1884, describing the mountain systems of the Perak region of the Malayan Peninsula based on his field observations.

## Summary

Tenison-Woods identifies three distinct mountain ranges in Perak: a main central range running north-south with peaks exceeding 7000 feet (including Gunong Riam at over 8000 feet); a coastal series of detached granite ridges trending north-north-east to south-south-west, including Titi Wangsu (nearly 7000 feet) and Gunong Inas (over 5000 feet); and a smaller eastern range of about twenty-five miles, also granite, with stratified limestone deposits on its lower shoulders (p. 135). He notes that the coastal ridges form a watershed between the coast and inland drainage, and that the peninsula's widening at its northern end is entirely due to this mountain system, of which Penang and the Dinding Group islands are part (p. 135).

Geologically, he observes that the first series has Palaeozoic schists, slates, and clays at its base, while the second has limestone, with tin deposits in both ranges derived from the older formation. He draws a close parallel between these tin-bearing granite systems and those of north-eastern Australia and Tasmania, suggesting they represent remains of a very ancient mountain system (p. 136). He also corrects the persistent error in recent authoritative works that Mount Ophir near Malacca (4360 feet) is the highest peak on the peninsula, listing several Perak mountains far exceeding that height (p. 136).
