# A collection of Malay proverbs

This short article by Mubin Sheppard documents the commissioning and creation of a new pulpit (mimbar) for the National Mosque (Masjid Negara), Kuala Lumpur, and profiles the Kelantanese woodcarver Latif Long who executed the work (p. 95).

## Summary

The article recounts how, in 1993, the Government decided to replace the original pulpit of the National Mosque with a new, larger one for the installation of the Yang Di-Pertuan Agong, Tuanku Jaafar. The redesign was entrusted to Dato Baharuddin bin Abu Kassim, the mosque's original architect, while the carving and decorative panels were assigned to Latif Long, described as the best-known Malay woodcarver in Peninsular Malaysia (p. 95).

The piece provides a brief biography of Latif Long, tracing his lineage as the seventh generation of a family of woodcarvers from Kampong Raja, Besut, and his early training in his father's workshop. It notes his first-prize wins at age sixteen in competitions organised by the National Museum and National Art Gallery, which led to commissions including a carved screen for the Prime Minister's office and a folding screen for the Malaysian Airline System reception office in London (p. 95). The new pulpit was carved from over three and a half tons of chengal timber, the hardest and most durable Malay timber, which Latif personally procured from the forest. The work occupied over six months, with Latif drawing every panel himself and supervising a workshop of young Malay carpenters in Kelantan. The article highlights that Latif re-introduced traditional leafy designs characteristic of Kelantan and Terengganu timber architecture to the pulpit's panels (pp. 95–97).
