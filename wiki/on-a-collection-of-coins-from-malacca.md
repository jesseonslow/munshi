# On a collection of coins from Malacca

A 1905 article by R. Hanitsch describing a second collection of coins dredged from near the mouth of the Malacca river, presented to the Raffles Museum by the Hon'ble R. N. Bland, Resident Councillor of Malacca (pp. 212–214).

## Summary

The article reports on newly recovered Portuguese tin coins from the reigns of King Emmanuel (1495–1521) and John III (1521–1557), the earliest of which were likely struck by Albuquerque after the 1511 occupation of Malacca. The most notable finds are six large tin coins in two varieties (35–37 mm diameter, 37–45 g) bearing the inscription "NOSTRE SPES VNICA CRVX XPI" on the obverse and a partially legible reverse reading interpreted as "Semper Depulsor Deus," alongside three smaller tin coins (24 mm) with the abbreviated legend "NOSTRAE SPES VNICA." (pp. 212–214)

The remaining Portuguese specimens are catalogued by reign and cross-referenced to Hanitsch's earlier 1903 paper on the first collection from the same site. Approximately twenty-five Malay coins are also noted, one of which bears the Arabic inscription corrected here from "maliku 'l-'adil" ("the just king") to "mulk 'adil," meaning "full weight currency" or "legal tender," on the suggestion of Rev. Dr. Luerig. (pp. 213–214)
