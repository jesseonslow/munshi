# Settlement of a tribal dispute (North Borneo

This short note by R.H.W. Beresford-Peirse, then District Officer of Sandakan, documents the resolution of a decades-old head-hunting feud between the Rumanau Alab and Mangkahak peoples of the Kinabatangan River headwaters in North Borneo (p. 221).

## Summary

The account traces the dispute from its origin in head-hunting raids over seventy years earlier, through a partially completed *bangun* (compensation) settlement that was interrupted by a smallpox epidemic, to its re-ignition in 1951 when a drunken Mangkahak man refused to sit with Rumanau headmen at a Governor's party (pp. 221–222). Beresford-Peirse presided over the formal settlement proceedings at the Tamu Tahun at Tongod in May 1953, assisted by the D.A.D.O. and two *panglimas*, with eleven old headmen as advisers and a crowd of over two hundred onlookers (p. 222). After ten days of prior hearings and a three-hour session at Tongod, the parties agreed to a *bolodop* (river endurance) test to determine which side was lying about the death toll; the Mangkahak withdrew the challenge overnight and agreed to pay the reduced *bangun* list (pp. 222–223).

The note concludes with the *sumpah* ceremony, in which both sides swore never to fight again, never to raise the *bangun* question again, to permit inter-marriage, and to acknowledge allegiance to the Queen and Her Government, sealing the oath by killing a fowl on a *billian* post and smearing its blood over the post (p. 223).
