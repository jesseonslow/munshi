# Description of new genera and species of Hymenoptera taken by Mr. Robert Shelford at Sarawak, Borneo

P. Cameron published this extensive taxonomic monograph in 1903 in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society*, describing new genera and species of Hymenoptera from specimens collected by Robert Shelford at Sarawak, Borneo. The paper, spanning over a hundred pages, represents a continuation of Cameron's earlier 1902 contribution (Vol. 37) and constitutes one of the most comprehensive single-author treatments of Bornean Hymenoptera produced during the colonial period.

## Summary

Cameron's paper addresses the systematic description of an exceptionally rich assemblage of Hymenoptera from the Sarawak region, covering families ranging from the sawflies (Siricidae, Tenthredinidae) through the parasitic wasps (Cynipidae, Chalcididae, Evanidae, Stephanidae, Braconidae, Ichneumonidae) to the aculeate wasps (Mutillidae, Pompilidae, Sphecidae, Vespidae) and the bees (Anthophila). The central taxonomic problem is one of generic delimitation: Cameron erects approximately fifteen new genera, each justified by combinations of wing venation, abdominal morphology, and head sculpture that he argues distinguish them from established groups. His systematic framework draws heavily on the contemporary arrangements of Ashmead, Krieger, and Szepligeti, and he frequently positions his new taxa relative to these authors' synopses.

The narrative is organized by family and subfamily, with each new species described through detailed morphological diagnosis covering colouration, sculpture (puncturation, striation, reticulation), wing venation, and genitalia. Cameron provides comparative remarks linking new species to previously described taxa from neighbouring regions—Singapore, the Khasia Hills, Celebes, Java, and Ceylon—thereby constructing a picture of regional biogeographic continuity across the Malay Archipelago. Several species are noted as probable synonyms of Smith's earlier, often inadequate descriptions from the 1850s, and Cameron explicitly addresses the nomenclatural consequences of these identifications.

The paper also records several re-examinations of previously described species, including notes on the variability of *Odynerus 7-fasciatus* Smith and the first description of the male of *Xylocopa caerulea* Fabricius, which had been known only from females. Cameron's descriptions are characteristically dense in morphological detail, reflecting the Linnaean-era convention of exhaustive character enumeration rather than the more streamlined diagnostic style that would emerge later in the century.

### Key Findings

- Cameron erects approximately 15 new genera, including *Mesocynips* (Cynipidae, forming a new subfamily positioned between Ibaliinae and Cynipinae), *Megachalcis* (Chalcididae, from the Khasia Hills), *Elphea*, *Plesiobracon*, and *Sigalphogastra* (all Braconidae), *Aglaophion* (Ichneumonidae, Ophionini), *Cyanoxorides*, *Spiloxorides*, and *Lethulia* (Ichneumonidae, Xoridini), and *Ctenonomia* (Anthophila) (pp. 91–179).
- The paper describes approximately 90 new species, with the Braconidae (subfamilies Braconinae, Rhogadinae, Macrocentrinae, Agathidinae) and Ichneumonidae (Ophionini, Pimplini, Xoridini, Mesostenini, Joppii) receiving the heaviest treatment; the genus *Iphiaulax*/*Iphiaula* alone accounts for over twenty new species from Kuching and Matang (pp. 103–120).
- Collection localities are concentrated at Kuching, Matang (at elevations of 2,500–3,600 feet), Santubong (2,600 feet), and Trusan, with supplementary material from Singapore, Lingga, Java, Ceylon (Trincomali), and the Khasia Hills (pp. 89–181).
- Cameron identifies *Mesocynips insignis* as probably identical with Smith's "Cynips" *insignis* (Proc. Linn. Soc. 1857, p. 117) from Sarawak, and assigns *Macroyaster flavopictus* Smith (1857) from Singapore to *Epirhyssa*, demonstrating the taxonomic revision of Smith's early Malay descriptions (pp. 93, 134).
- The male of *Xylocopa caerulea* Fabricius is described for the first time, noted to differ from the female in having soot-coloured rather than blue pubescence and a narrower head; the specimen is 17 mm in length (pp. 179–180).
- *Rhyssa maculipennis* Smith, recorded from Kuching, is noted to vary in size from 20 to 27 mm with variable yellow markings on the thorax and black on the legs (p. 134).

### Conclusion

Cameron's definitive contribution is the establishment of a systematic framework for the Hymenoptera of Sarawak and the broader Malay Archipelago, demonstrating that the region harbours a fauna of considerable generic novelty relative to the Indian and Australian faunas. By positioning his new genera within the existing classification of Ashmead and Krieger while simultaneously exposing the inadequacy of Smith's earlier descriptions, Cameron lays the groundwork for the more refined biogeographic analyses that would follow in the early twentieth century.

## Context

- Cameron was a British entomologist based in the Straits Settlements who served as the de facto authority on Oriental Hymenoptera during this period; his descriptions reflect the colonial-era practice of naming species after colonial administrators and collectors (e.g., *Cyanoxorides Brookei*, *Salius robertianus*, *Odynerus Robertianus*, *Iphiaulax Shelfordi*).
- The primary material derives from the Sarawak Museum collection and Shelford's personal captures; Cameron also draws on specimens from the Khasia Hills (coll. Rothney), Singapore, and Ceylon (Yerbury), indicating the use of multiple institutional and private collections across the region.
