# Notes on the folk-lore and popular religion of the Malays

This is a brief note by R. O. Winstedt reporting a first-hand observation of the *bapua* (or *singketa*) mock-combat ritual performed before rice planting, with reference to C. O. Blagden's earlier description in JSBRAS Vol. 29 (1896).

## Summary

Winstedt recounts witnessing the combat at Johol, where two parties hurled *puar* rods—approximately 3½ feet long, thin, green, and straight, with a flat end derived from a pared root—across a gully at one another for about half an hour. A *pawang* opened the proceedings with an invocation, and the purpose was to expel evil influences from the fields before planting. He notes that *singketa*, as used in Selangor, is a more serious variant employing heavier weapons (*batang pisang*), and suggests the rods may be stems of a ginger-plant (p. 249).
