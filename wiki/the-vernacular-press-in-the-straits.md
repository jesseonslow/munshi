# The vernacular press in the Straits

This note by E. W. Birch, read at a meeting of the Royal Asiatic Society on 30 January 1880, provides a detailed account of the *Jawi Peranakkan*, the first Malay-language newspaper published in the Straits Settlements.

## Summary

Birch describes the *Jawi Peranakkan* as a weekly paper (published Mondays) with a circulation of approximately 250 copies, priced at 30 cents per copy or $5 per annum. He notes its transition from lithograph to type (obtained from England) after its first year, its non-hostile tone towards Government, and its literary significance in standardising the Malay written language. The paper's agents operated across London, Penang, Malacca, Klang, Johor, Deli, Padang, Batavia, and Sarawak (p. 193).

The bulk of the note is a detailed resume of the issue dated 12 January 1880, which Birch uses to illustrate the paper's editorial character: market quotations, advertisements, a Government Circular on the Malay College at Telok Blanga, news of the death of the Datoh Klana of Sungai Ujong during his return from Mecca, commentary on the Moar disturbances, a correspondence column, extracts from the *Rangoon Times*, *Straits Times*, and *London and China Express*, and Indian telegrams (pp. 193–195). Birch also observes the paper's practice of rendering English journalistic terms in Malay script (e.g., *Mualif* for "Editor") and mentions a companion publication by the same company: a Mahomedan and English Comparative Calendar sold at ten cents per copy (p. 195).
