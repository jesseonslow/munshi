# Discovery of a stone implement in Singapore

This short note by H. N. Ridley reports the discovery of a worked stone adze at Tanjong Karang, Singapore, and its presentation to the Museum by Lieutenant A. D. Cox (p. 141).

## Summary

The implement is a dark chocolate-brown granite axe, five inches long and nearly four inches across, with one end abruptly truncate and the other ground to a rounded cutting edge. A small coral attached to the surface indicated it had recently come from the sea, and Ridley traced its origin to below high water mark at Tanjong Karang, a small promontory of iron-stone on the West Coast of Singapore (p. 141). The uneven grinding of the edge led Ridley to identify it as an adze or *biliong* rather than a cutting axe (p. 142).

Ridley notes that this was the first worked stone of this class found in Singapore, though similar implements known as *Batu Linta* had been obtained in Perak and Pahang. He argues that, unlike the Pahang specimens made of a black igneous rock of uncertain provenance, the Singapore adze was likely made locally from granite available at Pulau Ubin or in the hills between Tanjong Karang and Toas (p. 142).
