# Notes on an infant maias

This is a short note by C. A. Gibson-Hill identifying and describing a previously unrecorded Dutch-Malay vocabulary compiled by Pieter Willemsz van Elbinck (Peter Floris) at Acheh in 1604, now held in the University of Cambridge Library.

## Summary

Gibson-Hill draws attention to an unfinished manuscript word-list that Linehan (1949) had overlooked in his survey of early Malay lexicography. The list, written in Dutch with Malay equivalents rendered in Jawi (Arabic script), is the earliest known attempt to compile a Malay vocabulary in that script. It is divided into three chapters: "on man and his limbs" (53 Dutch words, 51 with Malay equivalents), "on the four elements" (35 Dutch words, 18 with translations), and "concerning the city, the houses and the situation of Acheh" (15 Dutch words, no translations) (pp. 204–205).

The note traces Floris's biography—born in Elbing, East Prussia, of Dutch stock, he served the Dutch East India Company on the Coromandel coast and on a mission to Arakan before joining the English East India Company's seventh voyage on the *Globe* in 1611, where he appears as Peter Williamson Floris. He died in London in September 1615. The manuscript passed through the library of the Dutch scholar Erpenius to the Duke of Buckingham and thence to Cambridge, where it went unnoticed until Dr van Ronkel published the vocabulary with critical notes in 1896 (pp. 205–206).
