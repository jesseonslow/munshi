# Ornithological notes made in the Straits Settlements and the western states of the Malay Peninsula

This note by W. Linehan identifies and discusses a pair of tombstones at the royal grave-yard of *Makam Chondong* (or *Makam Tujoh Beradek*) at Pekan, Pahang, and argues that they belong to *Marhum Muda Pahang*, a ruler referenced in the Perak royal *Salasilah* (p. 171).

## Summary

Linehan reproduces a reading of the inscription on one of the stones, which identifies the occupant as "Marhum Muda Abdullah (?)" and contains a standard Islamic funerary formula. He then draws on the Perak *Salasilah* (J.S.B.R.A.S., June 1882) to reconstruct the figure's biography: a *Marhum Pahang* arranged the marriage of his son, the *Raja Muda* of Pahang, to a Perak princess; the son succeeded his father, begat two daughters, and upon his death was known as *Marhum Muda*. His widow and daughters were sent to Perak, where they were later carried captive to Aceh by Iskandar Muda (*Marhum Mahkota 'Alam*), and one daughter married Raja Sulong, grandson of *Marhum Kasab* of Siak, who was sent to Perak as Sultan Muzaffar (p. 171).

Linehan connects this to the *Bustanu's-Salatin*, which records that Sultan Iskandar Thani of Aceh sent tombstones to Pahang in 1638–1639 for his relatives' graves—stones known locally as *Batu Aceh*. He proposes that *Marhum Muda Pahang* is the same person as the *Raja Muda* in the Perak account, and that his father *Marhum Pahang* may be the Sultan Ahmad carried to Aceh in 1618, making Iskandar Thani a half-brother of *Marhum Muda Pahang*. He also resolves a chronological difficulty raised by Dr. Winstedt (J.M.B.R.A.S. Vol. X, pt. III, pp. 44–45) by distinguishing *Marhum Pahang* (the father) from *Marhum Mangkat di-Pahang* (the ruler who died in 1677), noting that *Marhum Muda Pahang* died before 1638 (p. 172).
