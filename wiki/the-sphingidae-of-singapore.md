# The Sphingidae of Singapore

A brief field note by Lieutenant H. J. Kelsall, R.A., describing the hawk [moths](./moths.md) (Sphingidae) observed in the vicinity of [Singapore](./singapore.md), with notes on their habits, preferred [flowers](./flowers.md), and a list of recorded species.

## Summary

Kelsall writes in an informal, personal tone, recommending entomology as a suitable outdoor hobby for those stationed in the Colony. He describes the general morphology and behaviour of Sphingidae—crepuscular habits, swift humming [flight](./flight.md), hovering at flowers to feed on nectar with their long proboscis, and sensitivity to weather and moonlight. He notes that several species produce a squeaking sound, that they are susceptible to internal fungal attack, and that specimens taken in Singapore tend to be larger than those described in Moore's *[Lepidoptera](./lepidoptera.md) of Ceylon* (p. 327).

The paper concludes with a species list organised by sub-[family](./family.md) (Sphingidae, Acherontinae, Smerinthinae, Chærocampinae, and Macroglossinae), followed by brief remarks on the commonest species, including *Sphinx convolvuli*, *Diludia discistriga*, *Acherontia medusa*, *Chærocampa nessus*, *Pergesa acteus*, and *Hemaris hylas*, the latter noted for its transparent wings and its [larvae](./larvae.md)'s damage to [coffee](./coffee.md) plantations (pp. 327–329).
