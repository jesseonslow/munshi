# Foreword {to the volume of the journal dedicated to Sir Richard Winstedt

T. J. Hardy's *Catalogue of Church Records, Malacca, 1642–1898* (1937) is a systematic inventory of the ecclesiastical manuscripts preserved in Malacca spanning the Dutch colonial period through the late British era. Hardy, who served as Colonial Chaplain in Malacca from August 1891, compiled this catalogue as a practical reference for researchers seeking to trace the continuous religious and administrative history of the Straits Settlements through its church archives.

## Summary

The catalogue documents the full arc of Protestant church record-keeping in Malacca, beginning with the Dutch Reformed Church registers of the seventeenth century and continuing through the Anglican records of Christ Church in the nineteenth century. Hardy organises the holdings by register type—baptisms, marriages, burials, services, and bishops' visitations—and provides for each item a description of physical condition, date range, and notes on content significance. The Dutch-period materials (Nos. 1–30) include baptismal registers commencing in 1642, a marriage book from 1648, and burial lists, alongside administrative correspondence with the Dutch East India Company's ecclesiastical authorities in Batavia. The transition to British Anglican record-keeping is marked by the consecration of Christ Church in 1838 and the appointment of the first chaplain under the Bishop of Calcutta in 1831.

The later portion of the catalogue (Nos. 31–76) covers the Anglican period in considerable detail, including the Christ Church Mission's work among Chinese converts from 1870, the Girls' School record book documenting the institution's relocation and funding fluctuations between 1859 and 1894, and the Register of Services which Hardy notes also functioned as a Banns Book. Hardy's annotations draw attention to gaps in the record caused by mutilation or loss, and he cross-references overlapping registers to reconstruct continuous coverage. Two appendices provide a continuous chronological list of registers by type and a complete list of chaplains—Dutch and English—officiating at Malacca from 1641 to 1898.

### Key Findings

- The earliest surviving church records in Malacca are baptismal registers dating from 1642, with marriage records from 1648 and burial records from 1650 (Appendix A, p. 22).
- Christ Church, Malacca was consecrated by the Bishop of Calcutta on 25 August 1838; the first chaplain under the Calcutta diocese was Josiah Hughes, appointed August 1831 (p. 13; Appendix B, p. 23).
- The first recorded Malay service at Christ Church took place on 26 September 1869, and the first Chinese convert was baptised on 28 April 1872 (p. 14).
- The old Christian burial ground at St. Anthony's Cemetery was closed after 1 May 1861 and replaced by a new ground at Bukit Serindeh (p. 16).
- The Girls' School, established at Kubu in 1859 by the London Missionary Society, was relocated to Tranquerah in 1869, closed in 1874 for lack of pupils, and reopened at the Parsonage in 1875; its government grant fluctuated between $300 and $600 during the 1870s–1880s (p. 17).
- Hardy's list of chaplains records 52 individuals serving in Malacca between 1641 and 1898, including himself from August 1891 (Appendix B, pp. 23–24).

### Conclusion

Hardy's catalogue establishes that Malacca's church records constitute a continuous, if imperfect, documentary series spanning over two and a half centuries of colonial administration, making them an indispensable primary source for the social, demographic, and administrative history of the Straits Settlements.

## Context

- Compiled by the Colonial Chaplain himself during his incumbency (1891–1897), the catalogue reflects an insider's knowledge of the physical archives and their gaps, with Hardy noting his own editorial decisions regarding overlapping or duplicated entries.
- The work was published as a reprint by the Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society, indicating its continued utility as a reference tool for researchers working with Malacca's ecclesiastical manuscripts.
