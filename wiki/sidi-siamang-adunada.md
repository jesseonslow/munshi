# Sidi, Siamang, Adunada

This short note by R. O. Winstedt examines three terms found in Malay charm-books and proposes Sanskrit etymologies for each: *sidi*, *siamang*, and *adunada*.

## Summary

Winstedt argues that *sidi* in Malay charms is not a personal name or the Arabic *saidi* ('lord') but the Sanskrit *siddhi*—meaning supernatural power or magical accomplishment. He supports this by showing that *sidi* balances *sakti* in parallel lines, and that it is used interchangeably with synonyms *sah*, *mustajab*, and *kabul* in the formulaic closings of charms invoking the shahada (p. 7–8). He further links the phrase *bëkat sidi tërjali sëndiri* to the Sanskrit *vastusiddhih* and cites Monier-Williams on the role of *siddhi* in Tantric literature (p. 8).

For *siamang* (the lar gibbon), Winstedt revisits his earlier derivation from Sanskrit *sia-manga* ('black-bodied') and notes that *sia* also means 'shadow, night' in Sanskrit, suggesting the pawang's metaphorical use of *siamang bulan* may be a survival of this older semantic field (p. 8). Finally, he proposes that *Adunada*, the name of a spectre huntsman's companion in a charm, is a corruption of Sanskrit *anunada* ('echo'), an apt epithet for a spectral follower (p. 9).
