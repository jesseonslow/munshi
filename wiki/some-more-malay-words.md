# Some more Malay words

This short note by R. O. Winstedt proposes etymological derivations for a series of Malay words, drawing primarily on Sanskrit, Persian, Portuguese, and Dravidian sources (p. 135).

## Summary

Winstedt addresses words from multiple semantic fields. Among the more speculative suggestions are *malini* (from Skt. 'garlanded') in the *Sējarah Mēlayu*, *siamang* (from Skt. *syama* 'black'), and *kasi* 'to give' (possibly from a form of 'cash') (pp. 135–137). He also treats place-name identifications such as Kedah as *Zamin Turan* and Perak as *Kustan Zorian*, attributing these to romantic or unintelligent identifications with locations in Persian or *Hikayat* texts (p. 135).

The remaining entries cover everyday vocabulary: *endul* and *andor* (both traced to Skt. *hindola* 'swing'), *bērahi* (Skt. *virahi*), *bahara* (Skt. *bhara*), *mas* (Skt. *masha*), *mēngkona* (Skt. *matkuna*), *bangsal*, *baldi*, *serambi*, *chukai*, *chunam*, *chita*, *kendi*, and *misru*, with references to Wilkinson, Yule and Burnell, and other trade dictionaries (pp. 136–137).
