# Indra and Saktimuna

This short note by R. O. Winstedt, published in JMBRAS Vol. 23 (1950), addresses two topics: the etymological and mythological connections between the Malay legend of Saktimuna and the Rigvedic Indra, and a brief acknowledgment of Sir Roland Braddell's superior identifications of Takola and Langkasuka.

## Summary

Winstedt traces the Malay legend of the first ruler of Minangkabau slaying Saktimuna (variously "Serpent of wondrous power" or "Water Giant") to the Rigvedic account of Indra, the thunder-god, who cleaves a cloud-mountain to release waters, as explained by Hocart in *Kings and Councillors* (p. 151). He notes that early Malay rulers claimed to be incarnations of Indra, Lord of Meru, and that the legend's association with a prince who descended from the sky and improved the rice-crop confirms the weather-god connection (p. 152).

In the second note, Winstedt concedes that Sir Roland Braddell's identifications supersede his own earlier views: accepting Trang rather than Takuapa as the site of Takola, and agreeing that Langkasuka was essentially an east coast state (p. 152).
