# Grasping the nettle: first successes in the struggle to govern the Chinese in Malaya

This short note by H.G. Quaritch Wales responds to S.J. O'Connor's re-dating of the Takuapa Viṣṇu from the sixth century to the late seventh or eighth century, as proposed in O'Connor's article "Satingphra: an expanded Chronology" (JMBRAS Vol. XXXIX, pt. 1, 1966).

## Summary

Wales argues that O'Connor's reclassification of the Takuapa statue among the later series of long-robed "mitred Viṣṇus" of the Malay Peninsula and Chen-la is untenable on technical grounds. The Takuapa figure is distinguished by its exceptionally fine plastic qualities and by the fact that its upper arms are entirely free and in the round, without the supporting framework of stone reserves joining them to the mitre—a device characteristic of the later series, where Dupont and others have documented such lateral ties on undamaged examples (p. 153). Wales notes the contradiction in O'Connor's position: he admires the Takuapa's technical excellence yet groups it with statues whose "lack of technical facility" he himself has acknowledged (p. 154). Wales maintains Dupont's original assessment of close Pallava affinity and sixth-century dating, while accepting that the Satingphra torso, stylistically a little later, provides a valuable connecting link to the subsequent series (p. 154).
