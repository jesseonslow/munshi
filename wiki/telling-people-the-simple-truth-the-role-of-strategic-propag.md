# ‘Telling people the simple truth’: the role of strategic propaganda in the Malayan Emergency

This is a brief philological note by R. O. Winstedt, published in the *Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* (April 1917, No. 75, pp. 49–50), in which he examines the meanings and etymologies of several rare Malay words.

## Summary

Winstedt addresses eight lexical items—*kutaha*, *nakas*, *turap*, *tētērapan*, *kop*, *biram*, *ganteh*, and *Seri Menanti*—drawing on Wilkinson's *Seri Menanti* and Achinese parallels to clarify or correct their meanings. He argues that *tētērapan* is connected to *turap* ('to dress, plaster, line'), that *kop* denotes any dome or cupola (not merely the cupola of an elephant's howdah), and that *biram* refers to a mythical two-headed snake, which explains the Malay puzzle-ring *chinchin patah biram* (p. 50). For *ganteh*, he establishes the meaning 'thick in the centre; barrel-shaped; round' from folk-tale and Wilkinson's text, noting that a later Malay philologist had misread the word as *ganti* (p. 50). Finally, he proposes that the toponym *Seri Menanti* (seat of the Yamtuan of Negri Sembilan) is more plausibly derived from *seri menganti* ('waiting-hall') in Javanese palace architecture than from the folk etymology involving rice (p. 50).
