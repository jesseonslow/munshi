# Spolia mentawiensia. Reptiles and amphibians

This is a note by C. B. Kloss, published as part of the "Spolia Mentawiensia" series reporting on the 1924 expedition to the Mentawi Islands, covering two topics: the Onychophora (Peripatus) of the Malay Peninsula and the botanical results of the expedition as reported by Mr. Ridley.

## Summary

The first section (Records of the Raffles Museum, No. 21) reports on two *Peripatus* specimens in the Raffles Museum, determined by Professor W. J. Dakin as *Eoperipatus horsti* Evans (from 16 miles west of Johore Bahru, at sea level) and *Eoperipatus weldoni* Evans (from Kedah Peak, 3500 ft., in dead wood). Kloss notes the lowland versus montane habitat distinction between the two species and discusses the taxonomic status of *E. butleri* Evans, which neither Dakin nor Austin Clark appears to recognise (p. 263).

The second section summarises Mr. Ridley's botanical report on the 1924 Mentawi Islands collection, which yielded one new genus (*Polycycliska*) and fifty-six new species and varieties. Kloss highlights the biogeographical significance of the islands as the only deep-water province of the Malaysian subregion, noting that despite this, the flora shows no marked peculiarities compared to the rest of the region, likely due to the islands' low elevation (not exceeding 1,500 ft.) and small size. He also criticises the practice of naming new Mentawi species *sumatrensis* or *sumatrana* when they are not known from Sumatra (pp. 263–264).
