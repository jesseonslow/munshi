# Supplying Coal and Eradicating Piracy: The Royal Navy in the Straits of Malacca, 1833–1880

## Abstract

Tasked with protecting British interests in Asia, Britain’s Royal Navy had to deal with rampant piratical activity along the sea lanes in southeastern Asia, and the Straits Settlements played a central role in supporting the Royal Navy’s activities. The advent of steam-powered vessels gave the Royal Navy a significant advantage in the mid-19th century, but to patrol the vast area stretching from the Bay of Bengal to the South China Sea and deal with piracy there, the navy needed strategically placed coaling stations. This article looks at the establishment of a coaling facility at Singapore and trials there of Labuan coal, and subsequent naval operations against piracy.
